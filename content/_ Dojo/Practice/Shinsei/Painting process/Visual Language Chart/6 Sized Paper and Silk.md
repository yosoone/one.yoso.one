# Sized Paper and Silk

**Shinsei (心性, read _shinshō_): original mind**, "the unchanging essence of mind."[^1] Every entry ends with one question: _**how does this reveal original mind?**_

**Core idea:** _sizing_ seals the fibres so ink and water **rest on the surface** instead of sinking in. Where absorbent washi records the act at once and for good ([[4 Absorbent Washi]]), a sized ground **waits**. It allows fine line, layering, mineral pigment and gold. Choosing a sized ground means choosing **patience and precision**.

## Sizing

| Term | Reading | What it is | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alum-glue size** 礬水 | _dōsa_ | Animal glue (_nikawa_ 膠) and alum dissolved in water, brushed over paper or silk[^2] | Ink and pigment stay on top; edges stay crisp |
| **Sizing the sheet** 礬水引き | _dōsa-biki_ | Applying _dōsa_ with a wide brush, often in several thin coats | More coats give less absorbency |
| **Raw / half-sized / sized** 生・半熟・熟 | _ki / hanjuku / juku_ | The Chinese grading of _xuan_ paper by degree of sizing[^3] | A range from spontaneous to controlled |

## Grounds

| Ground | Reading | Behaviour | Character | Use when | Original mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sized washi** | _dōsa-biki washi_ | Ink sits on top; washes can be layered | Calm, controllable | Layered washes, pigment, gold | A surface that waits |
| **Hemp paper** 麻紙 | _mashi_ (e.g. _kumohada mashi_ 雲肌麻紙) | Strong, takes heavy sizing and pigment | Soft cloud-like texture | Large _nihonga_-style works[^4] | Strength that stays quiet |
| **Half-sized paper** | _hanjuku_ | Some bleed, some control | Between spontaneity and care | Works that combine gesture with detail | Freedom inside form |
| **Painting silk** 絵絹 | _eginu_ | Stretched on a frame and sized; ink glides; very fine line possible | Soft sheen, luminous, precious | Devotional images, fine line, gold | Light held inside the weave |
| **Painting from the back** 裏彩色 | _urazaishiki_ | Colour applied to the reverse of silk shows softly through the front | Glowing, veiled colour | Depth without heaviness[^5] | Light that comes from behind |

## What a sized ground makes possible

| Technique | Reading | Carries | Why it needs sizing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain line drawing** 白描 | _hakubyō_ | Clarity, restraint | Fine lines stay sharp |
| **Layered washes** 重ね塗り | _kasane-nuri_ | Depth, time | Each layer can dry before the next |
| **Pooling-in** たらし込み | _tarashikomi_ | Chance within control | The pool needs a surface that holds water long enough to form rings[^6] |
| **Mineral pigment** 岩絵具 | _iwa-enogu_ | Colour, weight, permanence | Glue binds the pigment to the surface |
| **Gold leaf, cut gold, gold paint** | _kinpaku, kirikane, kindei_ | Light, sacredness | Gold needs a smooth, sealed surface (see [[7 Gold and Indigo]]) |

## Absorbent vs. sized

| | Absorbent washi | Sized paper / silk |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | One moment | Many sessions |
| **Correction** | None | Limited: layering, lifting |
| **Ink** | Sinks in | Rests on top |
| **Edge** | Bleeding, feathered | Crisp, controlled |
| **Mood** | Spontaneous, final | Contemplative, devotional |
| **Original mind** | Mind acting without hesitation | Mind as a clear surface that reflects |

## Care

| Point | Note |
|---|---|
| **Aging** | Silk darkens and becomes brittle over centuries; alum in the size can speed this up[^7] |
| **Humidity** | Glue-based size softens in damp weather, so store and work with care in humid seasons |
| **Mounting** | Silk paintings need backing and are usually mounted as hanging scrolls (see [[9 Placement and Direction\|The Scroll]]) |

## Meaning

The Chan tradition compares the mind to a **mirror**. Shenxiu's verse asks that it be wiped clean so no dust gathers; Huineng answers that there was never any dust to land on.[^8] A sized ground behaves like the first verse: a smooth surface that shows what is placed on it. Absorbent paper behaves more like the second: there is no surface to keep clean, only the act.

**Statements:** "The sized ground waits." · "On silk, the light is inside the weave." · "Patience is also a trace."
