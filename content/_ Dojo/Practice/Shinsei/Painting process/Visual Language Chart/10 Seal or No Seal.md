# Seal or No Seal

**Shinsei (心性, read _shinshō_): original mind**, "the unchanging essence of mind."[^1] Every entry ends with one question: _**how does this reveal original mind?**_

**Core idea:** _rakkan_ (落款) is short for _rakusei kanshi_ (落成款識), "the mark made on completion." It is the signature and/or seal an artist adds when a work is finished.[^2] It can also include a pen name, a date, a note on the circumstances, or a poem.[^3] The red seal is often the **last mark placed and the only colour** in an ink work, so it is a compositional decision as well as a signature. Leaving it off is a decision too.

## What a _rakkan_ can include

| Element | Reading | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Signature** 署名 | _shomei_ | The artist's name or pen name, written with the brush |
| **Seal** 印 | _in_ | A carved stamp pressed in red paste |
| **Date** 年月 | _nengetsu_ | When the work was finished |
| **Colophon** 識語 | _shikigo_ | A note on the circumstances of the work |
| **Poem** | — | Verse that answers the image[^3] |

## Kinds of seal

| Seal | Reading | Look | Usual role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name seal** 姓名印 | _seimei-in_ | Often white characters on red (白文 _hakubun_) | Identity |
| **Pen-name seal** 雅号印 | _gagō-in_ | Often red characters on white (朱文 _shubun_) | The artist's working name |
| **Leading seal** 関防印 / 引首印 | _kanbō-in_ / _inshu-in_ | Usually oblong | Opens the work, often at upper right |
| **Free seal** 遊印 | _yūin_ | Any shape, carrying a phrase or image | Mood, a motto, balance |

These categories are standard practice; see Source Status.[^4]

## Placement

| Principle | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Where** | At the end of the work or in empty areas of the picture.[^3] In vertical works, usually low on the left, after the signature |
| **Not centred** | The seal is rarely centred; it balances heavy strokes or open space[^5] |
| **Size** | Small relative to the work; it should close the composition, not compete with it |
| **Colour and texture** | The red hue, the shape and the texture of the impression all affect the balance[^5] |
| **Hidden seal** 隠し落款 | _kakushi rakkan_: the signature or seal is worked into the picture, hidden in a tree or rock[^3] |

## Options for Shinsei

| Option | Where the mark goes | Carries | Use when | Original mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Seal on the front** | In the work, as the final accent | Completion, a red point of life | When the composition needs a closing weight | The self made small, as a witness |
| **Signature only** | Brush-written name, no seal | Quieter, still personal | When red would disturb the monochrome | The hand, without the stamp |
| **Hidden seal** | Worked into the image | Presence without display | When the mark should be found, not seen | Present but unseen |
| **Seal on the back** | Reverse of the work or backing | Record without intrusion | Minimal, meditative works | Identity kept out of view |
| **Seal on the box** | The inscribed lid (_hakogaki_ 箱書)[^6] | Identity carried by the box | Scrolls stored and shown by season | The work stays empty; the name rests outside it |
| **No seal** | Nowhere | Anonymity, openness | When any mark would take away from the simplicity | No self (無我 _muga_) |

## Meaning

A seal says _someone made this_. Leaving it off lets the work stand without an author in front of it, closer to the Buddhist teaching of no-self (_muga_, Pali _anattā_).[^7] Placing a small seal at the edge can say the same thing another way: the self is present, but small, and it comes last.

In practice, a work that may be sold or exhibited still needs some record of authorship. A seal on the back or on the box keeps that record while leaving the front empty.

**Statements:** "The seal is the last mark, not the main one." · "No seal: the work stands without an author in front of it." · "The self, made small, comes last."
