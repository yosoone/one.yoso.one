# The Scroll: Format, Placement and Direction

**Shinsei (心性, read _shinshō_): original mind**, "the unchanging essence of mind."[^1] Every entry ends with one question: _**how does this reveal original mind?**_

**Core idea:** the scroll is the core Shinsei format. The format decides **how the viewer meets the work**: seated or standing, all at once or over time, as a vertical axis or a horizontal path. Within it, placement and direction decide **where the eye enters, where it rests and where it leaves**. A scroll also has a life the viewer doesn't see: it is mounted, rolled, stored and brought out again with the seasons. The full reference on anatomy, proportions, formats and care is in [[Kakejiku]]; the seal, the last mark placed, is in [[10 Seal or No Seal]].

## Formats

| Format | Reading | Shape | How it is viewed | Carries | Use when | Original mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hanging scroll** 掛軸 | _kakejiku_ | Vertical | Hung in an alcove (_tokonoma_), often seated; changed with the season | Axis between heaven and earth; presence | The core Shinsei format | A still axis to return to |
| **Handscroll** 絵巻物 | _emakimono_ | Horizontal | Unrolled in sections, right to left, at a table[^2] | Time, journey, sequence | Works that unfold like a practice | Awareness moving through time |
| **Folding screen** 屏風 | _byōbu_ | Wide, in panels | Standing or seated; zigzag catches the light | Environment, space | Large gold or mist fields | Mind as the space you sit in |
| **One-line scroll** 一行物 | _ichigyōmono_ | Tall and narrow | Tea room alcove | One phrase, one breath | A single stroke or word | One thought, fully present |
| **Square board** 色紙 | _shikishi_ | Small square | Hand-held or framed | Intimacy | Studies, gifts | A small window |
| **Framed work** 額装 | _gakusō_ | Any | Wall, standing | Permanence, gallery | Exhibitions | (Fixed, not rolled away) |
| **Unmounted sheet** まくり | _makuri_ | Any | Before mounting | Openness, unfinished state | Storing or later mounting | Not yet placed |

## Direction

| Direction | Reading in Japanese space | Carries | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top → bottom** | Order of writing and of vertical scrolls | Descent: light or blessing coming down | Vertical works |
| **Right → left** | Order of reading and of handscrolls | Forward movement, time passing | Handscrolls, sequences |
| **Left → right** | Against the reading flow | Return, looking back | A counter-movement |
| **Rising stroke** | Upward | Aspiration, growth | Stems, flames |
| **Falling stroke** | Downward | Rest, grounding, letting go | Rain, petals, roots |
| **Towards the empty side** | Open space ahead of a form | Expectation, openness | See [[8 Yohaku and Ma]] |

## Placement

| Principle | Reading | What it does | Original mind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three distances** 三遠 | _san'en_: high (高遠), deep (深遠), level (平遠) | Three ways to build distance in landscape: looking up, looking through, looking across[^3] | Three ways of seeing the same mind |
| **Off-centre weight** | _fukinsei_ | Main form away from the centre; open space opposite | Nothing fixed at the centre[^4] |
| **High emptiness** | Upper part left open | Sky, breath, room for an inscription | Openness above |
| **Grounded base** | Weight low in the format | Stability, rest | The resting place |
| **Inscription** 賛 | _san_ | Poem or phrase in the upper space; the painting and words answer each other | The word as another trace |
| **Signature and seal** 落款 | _rakkan_ | Small, placed at an edge to balance the composition (see [[10 Seal or No Seal]]) | The self made small |

## Mounting a Shinsei scroll

The full reference on hanging-scroll anatomy, proportions, mounting formats, display and care is in [[Kakejiku]]. What matters most for Shinsei:

| Point | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Choosing the grade** | Mountings are graded formal, semi-formal and informal (_shin–gyō–sō_ 真・行・草). The formal Buddhist mounting (_butsu-hyōgu_ 仏表具) is reserved for sacred subjects such as Buddhist images and sacred names.[^5] For abstract Shinsei works, the general-purpose semi-formal mounting (_yamato-hyōgu_ 大和表具) is the natural default; the informal tea mounting (_chagake_ 茶掛) suits small works for a meditation or tea room.[^6] |
| **Proportion** | A standard mounting carries about twice as much above the work as below it, so the eye settles slightly high, suited to seated viewing.[^5] |
| **Media** | Mounting wets the whole back of the work. _Sumi_ on washi is ideal; heavy, thick or water-sensitive media are a risk. Tell the mounter the materials before work starts.[^7] |
| **Margin** | Leave a border of paper around the painting for the mounter to work with, and agree it before painting.[^7] |
| **Fabric** | The finest cloth goes closest to the work (the _ichimonji_ 一文字 bands). Bring the work to the mounter, ideally in the light it will hang in.[^7] |
| **Box** | Order the paulownia box with the scroll; the inscribed lid (_hakogaki_ 箱書) is part of the work's identity.[^7] |
| **Signature and seal** | See [[10 Seal or No Seal]]. |
| **Printing** | For printed washi scrolls, suppliers and prices are in [[Kakejiku - Printing and Suppliers]]. |

## The scroll in a room

| Point | Note |
|---|---|
| **Scroll first** | In the tea tradition, the scroll is called the most important utensil in the room[^8] |
| **Seasonal change** | Scrolls are rotated through the year, often two to four times; this also protects them, since continuous display is what damages scrolls[^7] |
| **Eye level** | _Tokonoma_ scrolls are seen from a seated position, slightly below the centre of the work |
| **Rolled away** | A scroll rests rolled up most of the time, like practice returning to stillness |

## Meaning

A hanging scroll is a **vertical axis**: something to sit in front of and return to. A handscroll is **a path**: something walked through, section by section. Within the format, direction gives the work a movement and placement gives it a resting point: weight placed low grounds the work, and open space above leaves room to breathe. For Shinsei, the hanging scroll suits original mind as a place to return to, and the eye should come to rest there, as the mind does.

**Statements:** "The format is how the viewer meets the work." · "Top to bottom, the light comes down." · "Nothing is fixed at the centre." · "A scroll lives rolled and unrolled, like practice."
