# Sumi Splash with Gold Leaf

## The Work

A hanging scroll — _kakejiku_ (掛軸). At the centre, one splash of _sumi_ (墨, ink), arrived in a single gesture. A touch of _kinpaku_ (金箔, gold leaf) where the light gathers.

That is the whole of it.

## What the Splash Carries

The splash is the moment awareness breaks into form. _Sumi-e_ (墨絵, ink painting) holds this as its oldest teaching: the brush meets the paper once, and whatever arrives is what there was.[^1] The mark is the mind, visible.

It arrives whole. Full saturation at the core, edges soft where water carries the pigment outward, sharp where the brush met the paper's resistance. Everything about it is a record of a single breath.

In the Shinsei context this is _shinsei_ itself — original nature, showing its own face.

## What the Gold Carries

The gold is the luminosity that was always present, now knowable.

Gold leaf behaves differently from pigment. It waits for the light and gives it back. In a dim room, a leafed surface stays dark until the moment someone moves, and then it gathers what little light there is and releases it.[^2] This is exactly the function it serves here: the gold reveals what the sumi already holds.

So the gold consecrates rather than decorates. It is offering. It marks the splash as a gesture made _for_ someone.

## What the Space Carries

The field around the splash is _ma_ (間, interval) — emptiness as presence. It holds the mark, and it holds the viewer standing in front of it.

The scroll form completes this. A scroll lives rolled; it opens when someone chooses to open it. The room arranges itself around it for as long as it hangs, and then it returns to stillness.[^3] The work and the encounter share one duration.

## The Hand

The scroll's power lives in the gesture that reveals it.

With the viewer's hand, the scroll becomes encounter. The splash appears. The gold catches. Each pull downward is a fresh commitment to see what comes next — awakening enacted, moment by moment, breath by breath. This is the Bodhisattva movement: radiance turned outward, light returned to whoever is looking.[^4][^5]

Maker and viewer meet as one here. Both are _shokunin_ (職人, artisan) of attention.
