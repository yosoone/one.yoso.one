# Rainbow — Light Divided

## Place in the Series

Late, and deliberately. Monochrome belongs to the present self; the bright, loud palette belonged to a younger and more political one. Colour returning at the end of the series is therefore not a change of mind but the record of a passage — which only works if it arrives last.

This note exists so the ending does not migrate forward into the beginning.

## The Argument About Light

Three operations on one light, in sequence:

- **Ink is light subtracted.** The page is the light; if white is never added, every mark takes something away from what was already there.[^1]
- **A rainbow is light divided.** Nothing is added. White meets a boundary and shows the constituents it was carrying all along.
- **Gold is light returned.** Not pigment but reflectance — it waits for the light and gives it back to whoever is standing there.[^2]

Subtraction, division, reflection. The series does not need a new idea to reach the rainbow; it needs to finish the one it started.

## The Work

Not a picture of a rainbow. No arc, no band of seven colours — that image belongs to other traditions and would break the restraint the series is built on.

What the work shows is dispersion: light meeting an edge and separating.

- **Form:** _kakejiku_ (掛軸, hanging scroll), vertical, as with the other stations.
- **Placement of colour:** at the boundary, never in the field. Colour appears where the mark meets the paper's resistance, not as an area of its own.
- **Proportion:** colour is subordinate in quantity to the _sumi_ (墨, ink). The field stays _ma_ (間, interval).
- **Ground:** Japanese papers, softer tones, as the material note of 15 September has it.

## The Rainbow Begins Inside the Ink

The important technical point, and the reason this station can be started without leaving monochrome:

Sumi is not one black. Inks fall into blue-black families — _aozumi_ / _seiboku_ (青墨) — and reddish or brown-black families, _chaboku_ (茶墨). The chromatic character is strongest in dilution: the lighter the ink, the greater the difference in colour, and the paper's weight and surface change how it blots and reads.[^3]

So the first colour in the series need not be pigment at all. A single black, diluted across a gradient, already divides. Two inks of different soot on one sheet divide further. _Nijimi_ (にじみ, bleed) at the edge of a wet mark is where that division becomes visible.

This gives a clean order of work: dilution first, two sooted inks second, gold third, pigment only if the first three prove insufficient.

## Against the Easy Version

The rainbow is the most available spiritual image and the most easily cheapened, so the discipline matters more here than anywhere else in the series:

- No arc. No symmetry. No seven bands.
- If a viewer's first word is "rainbow," the work has failed.
- The aim is that they see white light behaving, and only afterwards understand what they were shown.

Restraint is not modesty here. It is the difference between a work that divides light and a poster about hope.

## Pairing With Gold

The rainbow divides; the gold returns. Hung facing one another, the two close the argument that the ink opened.

Where the gradations series ends in recognition — the witness seeing itself — this pair ends in light: separated on one wall, given back on the other.

## Undecided

- One work, or a facing pair with the gold.
- Whether pigment enters at all, or the station is made from diluted ink and _kinpaku_ (金箔, gold leaf) alone.
- Whether it closes the gradations series or stands as the close of the whole body of work.
