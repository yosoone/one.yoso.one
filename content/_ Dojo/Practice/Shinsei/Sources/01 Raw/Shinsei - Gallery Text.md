# SHINSEI 心性

### Mindful Creation — Works and Practice by Yoso

_Soul Carver · Reiki Master · Lay Monk · Miyazaki, Kyushu_

---

**Shinsei (心性) — mindful creation — is the spirit that breathes through every work gathered in this room.** Each brushstroke, each drop of ink and water, each carved line arrives as an act of meditation. The works move across tattooing and Irezumi, Sumi-e ink painting, digital art, haiku, and photography, bound together by a single inquiry: the cultivation of consciousness, the nature of the self, and the living possibility of harmony with the world.

Through practice and commitment, Yoso makes art that helps transcend the self — Soul, Body, and Mind as one.

---

**I · Origins — A Spiritual Life from the Start**

Yoso's path begins early. Raised among natural therapies, he meets meditation, yoga, and alternative medicine in childhood, open to spirit from the very first. At sixteen, an encounter with Reiki and acupuncture heals a deep depression and becomes the pivot of his life. From that moment, fine art and energy work join as inseparable vocations. He studies martial arts, moving through hard physical and mental disciplines toward the deeper intelligence of chi gong and meditation — the passage from force into flow that runs through all he does.

**II · The Western Arc — From Academia to Digital Activism**

Trained in classical Western fine art, Yoso spends years across digital art, motion graphics, live performance, and cultural activism. His work engages race, power, consumerism, and collective identity, reaching audiences at the Serpentine Galleries in London (in collaboration with Cao Fei), OFFF Festival in Spain, Krakow Film Festival, Lowlands Festival in the Netherlands, and exhibitions in Taipei, Brussels, and Madrid. Collaborators across these years include Nike, Diesel, DJ Rupture, London Elektricity, The Stanton Warriors, D-FUSE, and Addictive TV. The work is energetic, global, purposeful — and a deeper call keeps sounding.

**III · Japan — Where Practice Became a Way of Life**

The meeting with Japan proves decisive. Drawn by Nature, Shinto, Buddhism, and the Shaolin spirit, Yoso follows the soul's journey here, reuniting heart and hand, body and spirit, one breath at a time. Miyazaki offers the conditions he has long moved toward: genuine practice.

Japan gives him an entire civilisation organised around his own questions. Shinto's sense of presence in all things — the _kami_ alive in a stone, a river, an ancient tree — offers a way of relating to the natural world that feels ancient and urgently alive. _Kokoro_ (心), the word that gathers heart, mind, and spirit into one, becomes a compass. Zen's trust in direct experience, and the Bodhisattva vow to work for the liberation of all beings, shape his sense of what art and healing serve.

From haiku he absorbs the discipline of _correct seeing_: a single image held precise and wholly present. Bashō knew haiku as a _Way_ (_michi_ 道), self-cultivation through close attention to nature and language — and Yoso meets the same spirit in his daily practice. From _shokunin_ culture, the ideal of the craftsperson who devotes a lifetime to mastering and transmitting a practice, he finds his ethical ground: the spiritual and the practical held as one, excellence offered as gift.

Koyasan, the sacred mountain of esoteric Buddhism, deepens his bond with ritual, lineage, and the hidden dimensions of practice. The teachings of Dzongsar Khyentse Rinpoche and the Bodhisattva tradition — above all the Four Great Vows, whose power lives in their deliberately boundless scale — orient his heart toward practice for the benefit of all.

**IV · Soul Carver 彫魂 — Tattooing**

In Japan, tattooing reveals itself as the practice that gathers everything together. Rooted in the Irezumi and Horimono tradition, whose spiritual lineage reaches back centuries, tattooing is at once craft, healing, and meditation. The living medium asks for absolute presence. Every line is a commitment.

He calls it _soul carving_: work at the threshold of the seen and the hidden, of surface and depth. Dragons, waves, botanicals, sacred geometry, animals — symbols drawn from Buddhist iconography, Shinto tradition, and the natural world, each carrying layers of meaning that reach far beyond decoration toward transformation. Love for people, the cultivation of spirit and compassion, and a quest for integrity draw him to the way of the ink: the intimacy of close encounter, the attention a living medium requires.

His guiding question, carved into the practice as a koan: _Is this pure?_ Pure as alignment with the source — clear, whole, true to love. If yes, move toward it. If still ripening, wait and purify further. The studio is a temple. Each session opens in the spirit of love and compassion.

**V · Daily Life — Ritual, Harmony, Simplicity**

Yoso's days in Miyazaki gather around practice: pre-dawn preparation, morning meditation, the Heart Sutra, chi gong, gratitude. The rhythm stays deliberate and spacious, an expression of the belief that how one lives and what one makes remain one and the same.

The southern countryside feeds the work continually — the quality of light, wind in the bamboo, the sound of cicadas, firefly season, the shifting moods of the Pacific coast. Nature teaches; harmony renews itself moment to moment. Each evening closes in gratitude: all prepared for the morning, the mind brought home to the heart, ready to celebrate tomorrow.

Here Sumi-e, writing, drawing, and photography become acts of listening as much as expression. _Shinsei_ (心性) — mindful creation — is the spirit behind it all: shaping the formless, discovering pattern in abstraction and in the spontaneous arrangement of a natural flow, something that speaks from beyond the surface. He moves toward the edges of consciousness, where discipline meets mystery and the light gathers at the centre — toward the peace and beauty held at the core.

Together these form **an ever-growing mandala of activities**: tattooing, Reiki, painting, writing, teaching — each petal opening from the same centre.

**VI · The Vow**

> _Awakening mind... that which is conscious... for the good of sentience._ — Yoso

The Bodhisattva ideal is the active orientation of the work. Yoso is a lay monk. The vow to serve all beings, renewed each day, shapes how he tattoos, teaches, heals, loves his family, and welcomes a guest to his studio. Those he has worked with and taught call him _Sensei_ — 先生, _one who came before_ — a word he holds with humility: precedence joined to responsibility, an encouragement to deepen the practice with integrity, to keep transmitting, to celebrate the flow.

> _"I speak from a human perspective. I invite everyone to explore consciousness in its most honest, rational, mystical, and magical forms. We often look outward for fresh ideas, inner peace, or stimulation — and we hold all we need within the experience of consciousness itself."_ — Y.

**VII · Looking Forward**

Yoso is growing a Dojo / Refuge / Temple in the Miyazaki countryside — a space rooted in nature, practice, and community, open for retreats, inspiration, and a direct encounter with a simpler, more intentional way of living. He spends most of the year in Miyazaki City, with regular travels to Europe and occasional visits beyond. A life and work in progress, he celebrates each encounter, and welcomes collaborations carrying genuine depth: tattoo commissions, custom artwork, creative consultations, and meetings with artists, producers, collectors, and institutions guided by integrity and the cultivation of the human spirit.

_The heart of the work resides in the cultivation of light._

One breath at a time. One bridge at a time.

Gassho. — Y.
