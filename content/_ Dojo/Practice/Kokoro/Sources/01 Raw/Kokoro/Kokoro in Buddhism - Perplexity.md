# Kokoro in Buddhism

In Buddhist contexts, _kokoro_ (心) refers to a "heart-mind" concept that holds mind, feeling, intention, and essence together rather than separating emotion from cognition. Academic study traces how Buddhist influence transformed _kokoro_ from its early Shinto roots into a broader spiritual and ethical ideal.[^1]

## Academic Framing

In the academic literature on Japanese literary and religious history, _kokoro_ becomes linked through Buddhist interpretation with interdependence and the inability to isolate a single fixed centre of experience. The self cannot be pinpointed; _kokoro_ is not a stable thing but a dynamic field of arising.[^1]

The medieval variant _mushin_ (無心, "no-mind") reflects a Buddhist paradox: the mind is most authentic when it is not attached to a self-centred perspective. To cultivate _kokoro_ in the Buddhist sense is to cultivate this openness — an awareness that meets reality without grasping at a fixed point of view.[^1]

## Concise Meaning

In Buddhist studies, _kokoro_ is best understood as a dynamic heart-mind: the lived unity of awareness, feeling, and intention. It points beyond both "emotion" and "mind" in the modern psychological sense toward spiritual cultivation and awakened response to reality.
