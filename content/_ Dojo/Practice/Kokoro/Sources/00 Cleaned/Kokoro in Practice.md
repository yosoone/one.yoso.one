# _Kokoro_ in Practice

> **Scope.** The lived and contemplative thread, consolidated from the gassho, spirit, presence, and cell-honouring notes in `01 Raw`, plus the practice sections of the essays. The word itself is in [[Kokoro - The Word and Its Range]]; the doctrinal history is in [[Kokoro - Japanese Buddhism - Heart-Mind in Doctrine and Practice]]; the scholarly treatment of cultivation is in [[Kokoro - Cultivation - Merit and Energy]]. Yoso's own lines are marked **— Y**. See [[Kokoro - Consolidation Log]].

> **Why this note stays separate from the doctrinal note.** The Buddhism note is a history: it traces _kokoro_ through the schools and the centuries and rests on the scholarship. This note is a practice manual: it asks what the word asks of a person on a given morning. They cross-reference each other at every point where the history bears on the practice. They are not merged because the doctrinal note would drown the practice, and the practice — especially the sections in Yoso's own voice — would sit oddly inside a scholarly survey.

## 1. _Mushin_ — the open heart

The fullest flowering of _kokoro_ in practice is _mushin_ (無心, no-mind) — the same graph 心 read as _shin_, with the negative prefix. It describes a heart-mind fully present, clear, and unattached: wholly given to the moment, moving with natural spontaneity. The doctrinal lineage — _hongaku_ thought, Takuan's _Fudōchi shinmyōroku_, Hakuin's _Song of Zazen_, and the diffusion of the term through the arts of the way — is set out in the Buddhism note.[^1] Here, only the practical consequences.

The Buddhist paradox is that the mind is most itself when it is _not_ attached to a self-centred perspective. Takuan's image is exact: the mind that "stops" (_tomaru_) on anything is bound there; the unfettered mind rests nowhere and is therefore everywhere at once.[^2]

Two consequences for practice:

- **Union comes through release, not effort.** We unite in _kokoro_ by loosening the grip that holds us apart. The field becomes whole on its own; the work is to stop preventing it.
- **_Mushin_ is transmissible as care.** Japanese hospices have developed what they call _mushin_ care — "open-minded care" — in which the caregiver who has grown quiet within becomes a clear channel of presence for the dying.[^3]

## 2. _Makoto_ — sincerity as the mode of attention

In the Shinto sensibility, _kami_ — the sacred — is not separate from the world but saturates it. To approach the world with _makoto_ (誠, sincerity of _kokoro_) is itself a form of reverence. The heart-mind is the organ through which sacred presence is perceived and met.

> The heart-mind that meets the waterfall and the heart-mind that meets a friend are one and the same heart-mind.

Reverence, on this view, is simply _kokoro_ fully turned toward what is before it. The whole of spiritual life becomes a matter of attention: to give the world the quality of presence we usually reserve for what we hold sacred, and to discover that everything, met this way, returns the gift.

## 3. _Hana_ — the flower between two attentions

Zeami Motokiyo (c. 1363–c. 1443) made _kokoro_ the foundation of Noh. Richard Pilgrim's study of the term in Zeami's treatises identifies several distinct senses — the performer's mind in training, the mind that must be "forgotten" in performance, the mind of the audience, and the _kokoro_ that is the source of the play's deepest effect, the ground of _yūgen_ and of the charged stillness Zeami called the interval of no-action (_senu hima_).[^4] Two of Zeami's ideas bear directly on union:

- _**Kabu-isshin**_ (歌舞一心, "song-dance-one-heart") — song and dance as two halves of a single whole, offered with complete unity of heart and mind. Body, voice, and feeling move as one.[^5]
- _**Hana**_ (花, the flower) — what blooms in the living relationship between performer and audience. In the _Fūshikaden_, the flower is "what is interesting" (_omoshiroki_) and "what is novel" (_mezurashiki_) — but it is not a property of the actor alone. It opens in the meeting.[^5]

This is union made visible. Two people attending fully to the same moment become briefly a single field of awareness, and something flowers there that belonged to neither alone. Anyone who has sat in silence with another, or worked beside someone in wordless coordination, has felt it.

## 4. _Gassho_ 合掌 — hands in prayer

_Transcribed from a handwritten note, 7 September 2024._

Hands in prayer. Re-centring. Cultivating presence, awareness, consciousness.

**Morning practice.** Welcoming the new day with gratitude. Accepting that life will bring the people, events, and situations one can best serve — and that those are the same ones that make one grow. Life is in constant evolution; change is the only certainty. Losing a job, changing career, losing loved ones, newborns. A stranger in the present.

> Respecting others is not a matter of social status or circumstance. It is acknowledging and respecting the life within each one of us.
> **— Y**

> Cultivating the spirit is something you cannot buy — as a practice, as a please and a thank you. It warms the heart to be respected.
> **— Y**

Beyond duality. Honouring the space. "Self" and "others" as one flowing energy. **— Y**

**Humility** — cultivating a deep sense of humanity. **Gratitude** — showing respect to the other: life, spirits, spaces.

**In use.** At each encounter, we recognise each other. Try it; it is contagious. Be sincere. A way to thank someone when paying for something — gratitude offered _on top_ of the money, as a transfer of energy and respect, from the heart, not a transaction. If you are too shy to be seen bowing, you can visualise it in heart and mind as an intention to the visible and invisible world.

Deep respect to the person — and to the spirits attached to them, the invisible realm.

It began with martial arts, meditation, and reiki; as awareness develops, it becomes second nature. Show respect to the space as if entering a temple or the dojo.

> We are all connected. Space, air, separation — it is an illusion.
> **— Y**

A transcription slip worth keeping, alongside the line it grew from:

> Be gentle to life… it is a prayer to experience — not a problem to be solved.
> **— Y** (transcription variant, kept deliberately)

> Spiritual life is not a problem to be solved but a reality to be experienced.
> — Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan[^6]

> **Attribution note.** The shortened form — "Life is not a problem to be solved, but a reality to be experienced" — circulates everywhere under Kierkegaard's name, and the original handwritten note attributed it to him. It is not his. No such sentence appears in Kierkegaard's works, and Wikiquote has removed it as unsourced. The line is Radhakrishnan's, from a passage on religion as "a form of being, not having."

## 5. Honouring each cell

> Hands in prayer,
> I bow respectfully to each cell in my body,
> and to each cell in you.
> **— Y**

Joining the hands in prayer, we find our own centre. Uniting the mind and the heart, we embrace _kokoro_.

Once we become aware of the multitude of biological and chemical processes that sustain the human body — and recognise that the same processes apply to other beings, and on a global scale — we can choose to honour life on a cellular and energetic level.

> Breathe in…
> Breathe out…
> The energy that connects all forms…
> Infinite chains of chemical reactions…
> Trillions of cells living within…
> Who am I?
> **— Y**

**Practice.** Breathe in quietness. Join the hands at the heart. Honour the life within. Breathe deeply; let attention merge with the breath. Develop gratitude for the life we have been given. Accept the eternal cycle. Visualise life transforming with each breath. Cultivate appreciation for all phenomena. Embrace the mystery.

Being one. Becoming one. Microcosm and macrocosm.

## 6. The _ki_–_kokoro_ bridge

_Kokoro_ names the unified inner life. The question of _spirit_ is what animates it — and the traditions answer with a cluster of words that are almost, but not quite, the same thing.

| Term | Tradition | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| _ki_ 気 / _qì_ | Japanese, Chinese | Animating breath-energy flowing through all living things; _genki_ (元気) is "original ki" |
| _jing_ 精 | Chinese | Essence; the concentrated root from which _ki_ flows |
| _prāṇa_ प्राण | Sanskrit | Life force, vital breath, moving through _nāḍī_ and concentrated in _cakra_ |
| _spiritus_ | Latin | Breath, the animating principle; _spirare_, to breathe |
| _tama_ 魂 | Japanese | Soul; in kami practice, what is pacified and renewed rather than circulated |

**How they meet.** _Kokoro_ is the experiential centre where feeling, knowing, and will converge; spirit is the force that makes that convergence alive. Without _ki_, _kokoro_ is a concept; without _kokoro_, _ki_ is energy with nowhere to land.

In Zen, _mushin_ is the state where _kokoro_ is so clear and open that _ki_ moves through it without obstruction — like wind through an open room. The martial artist, the ink painter, and the tea master are cultivating the same thing: a _kokoro_ responsive enough to let spirit move freely through it.

In Reiki practice, the practitioner channels _ki_ through the hands, and the quality of that channelling depends on the quality of the practitioner's _kokoro_ — its openness, stillness, and intention. Technique is secondary to inner state.

**The scholarly anchor.** Yuasa Yasuo is the one modern philosopher who took _ki_ seriously as a category and tried to say what it is. His argument, developed across _The Body_ and _The Body, Self-Cultivation, and Ki-Energy_, is that the East Asian traditions treat the mind–body relation not as a given fact to be explained but as an achievement to be cultivated (_shugyō_, 修行), and that _ki_ names the medium in which that cultivation happens — neither purely physical nor purely mental, but the felt continuity between them. On Yuasa's reading, _ki_ is what makes _shinjin ichinyo_ (身心一如, body-mind as one) a practice rather than a doctrine.[^7]

**Inner alchemy.** Taoist _neidan_ (内丹) offers the most explicit map: _jing_ (essence, body) → _qi_ (energy, breath) → _shen_ (spirit, mind). _Shen_ here is closest to _kokoro_ — the luminous awareness that is both goal and instrument. The process rests on the recognition that agent, instrument, and patient are not separate entities but aspects of one reality.

**The practical implication.** Every practice that cultivates _ki_ — breath work, reiki, meditation, martial arts, ink painting, tea, the gassho bow — is also a practice of _kokoro_. And every deepening of _kokoro_ — more openness, more presence, more sincerity — is also a cultivation of spirit. They are not two trainings. They are one training approached from different angles.

> **Note on sources.** The table and the "how they meet" paragraphs are a working synthesis, not a scholarly comparison. The terms come from distinct traditions with distinct technical meanings, and the equivalences drawn here are heuristic. Yuasa is the exception — his is a philosophical argument, not a heuristic. For the historically grounded treatment of how _ki_, _kaji_, and _tama_ actually function in Japanese cultivation discourse, see [[Kokoro - Cultivation - Merit and Energy]] §4.

## 7. Coherence without fixity

Three images convert the anxiety of impermanence into a competence. Developed at length in [[Kokoro for a Liquid Reality]]; kept here in short form as practice instructions.

**The waterfall.** A stable, nameable form in which no single drop remains. Identity is achieved not by stopping but by the steadiness of the falling. Coherence need not come from permanence; it can come from the integrity of a pattern that holds while its substance flows.

**The ripple.** A stone meets the water and rings spread outward, touching what the stone will never see. In a world understood as one interresponsive field, every act sends rings across the whole — and awareness of this _is_ responsibility. We cannot control where the rings travel; we can choose the quality of the stone.

**The open hand.** _Mushin_ meets each instant freshly because it holds nothing over from the last and grasps at nothing in the next. The one who has stopped clutching at solidity is the one most at home in the flow.

> The drop joins the ocean by remembering it was always water.

## 8. The paradox of depiction

_From the unique note of 2 September 2026 — kokoro, shinsei, and dojo as overlapping circles._

- **Kokoro** — cultivation of the heart: development, understanding, manifestation.
- **Dojo** — practice: the dojo in the heart, the inner body, and the official Dojo.
- **Shinsei** — constant rebirth: awareness and meditation.
- **The centre** — a continued state of awareness; confirmation and openness.

A gradual evolution. A gradual understanding. Sometimes lost in translation. Lost in definition.

The ultimate language of the heart remains a mystery to cultivate. How would you depict and communicate the formless sensation of being one with all and beyond time?

> Through the breath and the mindset, a calm within a sea of vibration.
> **— Y**

Formless. Potentiality. Manifest in simplicity. Let it unfold.

_See also the note of 15 June 2026:_ the Bhagavad Gītā's teaching that there are no separate elements, only a connected energy field — mapped onto _kokoro_ itself.

## 9. The practice, in one paragraph

It asks that we feel and think with the whole of ourselves, letting intelligence stay warm and warmth stay clear. It asks that we keep this wholeness through an ordinary day — in how we listen, how we work, how we hold the cup and greet the morning. It asks that we meet others at the level of truth. And it asks for courage: to live open enough that the edge between self and world can soften into welcome.

To unite in _kokoro_ is the work of a lifetime and the gift of a single breath. We return to it the way we return to the breath in meditation — and the returning is itself the practice.
