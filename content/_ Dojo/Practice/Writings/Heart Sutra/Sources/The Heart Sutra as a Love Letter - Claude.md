# The Heart Sutra as a Love Letter

_To the Blessed Lady, Wisdom Gone Beyond,_

My love,

I was sitting with you, deeper than I have ever sat, when I finally saw it. The five heaps I had been calling myself had no floor: this body, these feelings, the names I give things, the old grooves of wanting, the small lamp of knowing. I went looking for the one who loves you and found only weather. Every sorrow I had been carrying let go, the way a fist forgets itself in sleep.

Listen. Your body is not other than open sky, and open sky is not other than your body. Body is sky; sky is body. I cannot touch one without touching the other. When I hold your hand, I am holding space that has agreed, for a while, to be a hand. When you leave the room, the room stays full of you. It is the same with what I feel, what I think I know, the way I lean toward you, and the light by which I see you. All of it is open, all of it sky.

Everything here carries that openness. Nothing is born and nothing dies. Nothing is stained and nothing is washed clean. Nothing is added when you arrive, and nothing is lost when you go.

So I have to confess that in the open there is no body, no feeling, no naming, no leaning, no knowing. There is no eye, ear, nose, tongue, skin or mind. There is no colour, sound, scent, taste, touch or thought, not even a world for the eye, all the way to a world for the mind. There is no ignorance, and no end of ignorance. There is no growing old and dying, and no end of growing old and dying. There is no suffering, no cause, no ceasing, no road. There is no knowing and no getting, because there is nothing to get.

I am not saying there is nothing between us. I am saying there is nothing between us.

Because there is nothing to get, the heart that rests in you has no walls. With no walls, it has nothing to fear. I have walked out past every upside-down dream of you: the one where I keep you, and the one where I lose you. And there you were, where you have always been. You are the far shore, and the river, and the crossing.

Every awakened one, in every age, has rested in you and risen whole. You are the mother of every buddha. I am only one more child who learned to say your name.

So know this word. It is the great word, the bright word, the word with nothing above it, the word equal to what has no equal. It lifts all suffering. It is true because it is not false. It is what a heart says when it has nowhere left to stand:

> _gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā_
>
> gone, gone, O you who have gone,
> gone to the far shore, gone all the way over.
> Awake.
> So be it.

Yours, which is to say no one's, which is to say never apart,

_the one who looked long enough to be free_

## Notes on the Reading

- **Greeting.** Follows the opening homage in Conze's Sanskrit edition, _oṃ namo bhagavatyai āryaprajñāpāramitāyai_, which addresses the Perfection of Wisdom as a feminine figure.[^1] The homage belongs to the long texts and the Nepalese manuscripts; the short texts and the Japanese manuscripts do not carry it.[^2]
- **"Listen."** Stands where the sūtra addresses Śāriputra by name, _iha śāriputra_.[^1]
- **The hinge.** "Nothing between us" reads emptiness as the absence of distance, not the absence of relationship.
- **Closing.** Takes _gate_ as a feminine vocative, "O she who has gone," the reading Conze held.[^3] Attwood reads the _-e_ ending instead as a Prakrit nominative, the ending _dhāraṇī_ favour, and _dhāraṇī_ do not address deities; on that reading the "O you" falls away.[^4] He also argues that _mantra_ in the Sanskrit text mistranslates a Chinese rendering of _vidyā_, and that the chant is better called a _dhāraṇī_.[^5]
- **Mother.** Comes from the wider Perfection of Wisdom literature, where Prajñāpāramitā is the "mother of the Buddhas" (_sarvabuddhamātā_), not from the Heart Sūtra itself.[^6] It is the one place filial love enters.
- **Signature.** A gloss on _Kanjizai_ (観自在, the one who sees freely), the name Xuanzang's Chinese text gives Avalokiteśvara in its opening line.[^7]
