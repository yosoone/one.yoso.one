# China: The Graded Body

A paragraph in [[Subtle Body Across Traditions]] makes four claims about the Chinese material. This note tests them against the scholarship. Two survive, one needs a date stamp, and one is quietly merging two different bodies.

The claims, unpacked:

1. The Chinese vocabulary does not oppose a subtle body to a gross one.
2. _Jing_ (精), _qi_ (氣) and _shen_ (神) are three densities of one continuum.
3. Internal alchemy (_neidan_, 內丹) refines upward through them.
4. The three _dantian_ (丹田) are where the work is done.

### Three geometries

The same claims, drawn three ways. Each geometry buys one thing at the cost of another, and the set is more useful than any one alone. The third sits further down, in §3, where it does its work.

| | **Linear** (above) | **Open ring** (above) | **Radiating** (§3, below) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asserts** | _jing_ → _qi_ → _shen_ as an ordered passage through three fields, against a cosmogony descending beside it | refinement as reversion — the same road travelled back, ending in an exit rather than a turn | density as distance from a source: one substance thinning inward, thickening outward |
| **Shows well** | the bodily order, belly → chest → skull; the chronology; the _niwan_ exception | that the terminus and the origin are one point; that the circuit is left, not repeated | 先天 and 後天 as the structure of the picture; that the grades have no boundaries |
| **Cannot show** | that the end _is_ the beginning — it has to be said in a caption | any bodily location, and any dating at all | anything sequential or located: no fields, no stages, no chronology |
| **Risk it runs** | picturing reversion as a climb, which is the misreading this note exists to correct | a ring reads as a cycle that repeats, which is a different doctrine — hence the break | solid rings would make it the Theosophical stack of bodies — the opposite claim — hence every boundary dashed |

The linear diagram is the evidence, the ring is the motion, and the radiating one is the ontology. Reversion is a shape; the dating and the anatomy are facts about where and when, and facts of that kind do not curve. The one thing worth watching in the ring is the gap: a closed circle would quietly import the _Cantong qi_'s cyclical fire phases into a soteriology whose whole point is that you stop.[^c]

## 1. No second body — this holds, and it is the important one

Nothing in the Chinese sources corresponds to _sūkṣma śarīra_ or _ochēma_: a discrete envelope you possess, that survives you, that can be counted as a second item. Pregadio's summary of the Daoist body distinguishes five operative models — cosmological, political, theological, natural, alchemical — and none of them is _a second body_.[^1] Each is the one body described under a different competence.

This is a real structural divergence and it is the thing most often flattened in comparison. It also means the English phrase does more damage here than almost anywhere else in the comparative field: "the Chinese subtle body" names a thing the sources do not have.

**Verdict: holds, and should be stated more strongly than the paragraph currently does.**

## 2. Three densities of one continuum — holds, with a caveat about who is speaking

The strongest evidence is older than Daoism as a religion and startlingly explicit. The _Neiye_ (內業, Inward Training), a rhymed meditation text generally dated c. 350–300 BCE and probably Jixia Academy material, defines the terms against each other:

> The vital essence \[_jing_]: it is the essence of the vital energy \[_qi_].[^2]

_Jing_ is _qi_ at a finer grade — said in so many words, in the earliest extant Chinese text on contemplative practice.[^3] Harold Roth's edition remains the standard treatment, and his later volume situates the text within a wider classical inner-cultivation lineage.[^4] The graded reading is therefore not a modern projection; it is native, early, and stated.

Three qualifications keep it honest.

**Qi is not energy.** Sivin's and Kuriyama's work has made the point at length: _qi_ in early sources is closer to vapour, atmosphere or exhalation than to any conserved quantity, and the Chinese medical body was built by attending to pulse and flow rather than to structure — producing an anatomy incompatible with the Greek one but internally coherent.[^5] "Densities of one continuum" is the right shape; "energy at different frequencies" is not.

**The registers differ.** In the medical corpus _jing_ is reproductive essence and _shen_ is the animating spirit that manifests in the complexion and the eyes — the _Lingshu_ defines _shen_ as what arises when the two essences of the parents clasp together, which is an embryological claim, not a rung on a ladder.[^6] The same three graphs are doing different work in medicine, in cosmology and in alchemy, and only the last makes them a refinement sequence.

**The slogan is late.** Calling the triad "the three treasures" (_sanbao_, 三寶) borrows a phrase that in _Daodejing_ 67 names three quite unrelated things — compassion, frugality, and not daring to be first in the world. The twentieth century then hardened the triad into a popular doctrine of vital economy through the _qigong_ movement, whose scale, vocabulary and scientific ambitions Palmer has documented; much of what now circulates as timeless Chinese teaching about _jing_ is downstream of that.[^7]

**Verdict: holds as early Chinese physiology, with the caution that "continuum" is a grading of one stuff, not a spectrum of one energy.**

## 3. Refining upward — holds for _neidan_, but _neidan_ is medieval, and the arrow is not really upward

This is where the paragraph needs a date.

**The term.** _Neidan_ becomes current in the late Tang or slightly later, and initially designates stages within a practice rather than a separate branch of alchemy; Baldrian-Hussein's article is the study of its origin and shifting usage.[^8] The earliest traces of the practice appear in works by Tao Zhi in the second half of the eighth century. The first clearly identifiable lineage is Zhong-Lü, ninth to tenth century; the Southern Lineage (Zhang Boduan, thirteenth century) and Northern Lineage (Wang Chongyang, 1113–70) follow.[^9]

**The three-stage sequence** — _lianjing huaqi_ (煉精化氣), _lianqi huashen_ (煉氣化神), _lianshen huanxu_ (煉神還虛) — derives from Southern Lineage doctrine.[^10] Practical manuals usually prefix a fourth, "laying the foundation" (_zhuji_, 築基), which restores the Three Origins before refinement proper can begin.[^11] So the ladder is Song-and-after, not ancient, and the _Neiye_ passage above is not an early statement of it. Two separate claims, a millennium and a half apart, that happen to point the same way.

**Neidan is not interiorised waidan.** The intuitive genealogy — external alchemy moves inside — is the one the specialists reject. Pregadio's account has meditation on the inner gods supplying the symbolic resources (a pantheon, circulating pneumas, an inner landscape), and the _Zhouyi cantong qi_ — traditionally attributed to Wei Boyang in the mid-second century but probably c. 450 CE or later — supplying the correlative cosmology that replaced them. The _Cantong qi_ explicitly criticised the earlier visualisation methods it borrowed from.[^12] Robinet's essay on what _neidan_ contributed that was genuinely new makes the same case from the doctrinal side.[^13]

**The arrow is reversion, not sublimation.** Refinement inverts the cosmogonic sequence: from multiplicity back through duality and Unity to Non-Being.[^14] Governing it is the precelestial/postcelestial distinction (_xiantian_ / _houtian_, 先天 / 後天), which the paragraph omits and which changes the picture. What is refined is not the ordinary substance. Liu Yiming (1734–1821) rejects postcelestial essence — semen — as any basis for practice at all, on the grounds that it "comes into being after one's birth"; the work is to borrow the postcelestial in order to return to the precelestial.[^15] "Essence into breath into spirit" describes a cosmological reversal staged in a body, not a fluid being distilled.

## 4. The three cinnabar fields — where two bodies get merged

This is the claim that needs the most work.

**The fields are late arrivals and they were not built for this job.** _Dantian_ first appears in two sources of 165 CE concerned with Laozi's deification — the _Laozi ming_ (Inscription for Laozi) and the Stele to Wangzi Qiao. The first detailed description of the lower field is in the third-century _Laozi zhongjing_ (Central Scripture of Laozi), which explains the "cinnabar" as the red colour of the innermost field rather than the mineral; the _Huangting jing_ (Scripture of the Yellow Court) — outer version late Eastern Han, inner version late third century — works the three fields extensively.[^16]

Their original function was theological. The fields housed deities: the Three Ones, and in the lower field the Red Child, an embryonic true self. Steavu's study of Daoist embryological discourse shows the lower field acquiring reproductive imagery precisely because it sits where gestation happens, and shows that early practitioners drew no line between a literal and a symbolic embryo — both were stated in the same cosmological idiom.[^17] The standard modern gloss, _lower field = seat of jing, middle = qi, upper = shen_, is an alchemical overlay on a pantheon.[^18]

**The upper field is in the head.** _Niwan_ (泥丸), the brain region, divided into nine palaces.[^19] This matters for the parent note, which lists _dantian_ among evidence that contemplative centres cluster in the trunk. One of the three does not, and the sequence deliberately ends there — the refinement moves belly to chest to skull. The convergence claim needs qualifying rather than dropping; it is not a counterexample to "mind in the heart" so much as a reminder that the Chinese scheme is not organised around a single seat at all.

**And these are not the meridians.** The paragraph says the _dantian_ are "where the work is done," which is correct for _neidan_ and misleading if it suggests one Chinese body. The channel-and-viscera system of the medical classics and the cinnabar fields of the cultivation literature are different maps with different histories, and they disagree — Wang Mu notes that medical and alchemical texts place the fields differently.[^20] Stanley-Baker goes further, arguing that "Daoist medicine" (_daojiao yixue_) is a twentieth-century neologism appearing nowhere in the pre-modern sources, and that the early Celestial Masters in fact suppressed acupuncture, herbs and moxibustion in favour of ritual confession — competitors, not colleagues.[^21]

So the divergence the parent note looks for _between_ traditions is already present _inside_ this one. That is a more useful finding than another convergence.

**The famous charts are modern.** The _Neijing tu_ (內經圖) survives through an 1886 stele at the Baiyun guan in Beijing and is probably a nineteenth-century composition; the _Xiuzhen tu_ (修真圖) is late eighteenth century, and Despeux identifies twenty-five versions, with earlier precedent in the tenth-century Yanluozi charts.[^22] They are magnificent and they are not evidence of an ancient anatomy.

**Verdict: holds for neidan. Rephrase so it does not imply a single Chinese body, and note that the upper field is cranial.**

## The deeper objection: is there an alchemical body at all?

The tradition's own most sophisticated writers say no, or something close to it. Liu Yiming distinguishes the "illusory body" (_huanshen_, 幻身) of flesh from the true body, and treats identification with the former as the basic error.[^23] Pregadio's reading of the alchemical vocabulary is that its terms are pointers to formless principles rather than descriptions of locations — the Mysterious Barrier is "neither internal nor external."[^24]

Taken seriously this is the sharpest thing the Chinese material contributes to the comparative question. Tantra builds a body; Vedānta inherits one; Sufism enters a world. Neidan, at its most rigorous, denies that the anatomy it describes in such detail is an anatomy — and keeps describing it anyway, because the description is the instrument. Whether that is evasion or precision is the live question, and it is the same question Kuriyama's method answers for medicine: these are disciplined ways of perceiving, and asking whether the object is "really there" is the wrong test.[^25]

## Suggested replacement paragraph

For the China section of [[Subtle Body Across Traditions]]:

> The Chinese vocabulary does not oppose a subtle body to a gross one — there is no second envelope to have, build or lose. It grades a single substance. _Jing_ (精, essence), _qi_ (氣, breath or vapour) and _shen_ (神, spirit) are densities of one continuum, and the grading is stated as early as the _Neiye_ (c. 350–300 BCE): essence is "the essence of _qi_." Internal alchemy (_neidan_), a medieval development whose three-stage sequence — essence into breath, breath into spirit, spirit into emptiness — comes from Song-dynasty Southern Lineage doctrine, refines back along that continuum. The motion is not sublimation but reversion: the cosmogonic sequence run backwards, from the postcelestial to the precelestial, ending not in a finer body but in emptiness. The three _dantian_ (丹田, cinnabar fields) are where that work is staged — lower belly, chest, and brain. They began as residences for inner deities in texts of the second and third centuries CE and were later reassigned as the seats of essence, breath and spirit; they belong to the cultivation literature, not to the channel-and-viscera body of the medical classics, which is a different map with a different history.

- **The upper field breaks the trunk pattern.** Worth carrying back into the "what converges" section of the parent note rather than leaving it as a silent exception. The honest version of convergence 3 is that contemplative centres cluster in the trunk _and_ that the traditions which stage a sequence run it toward the head — Chinese _niwan_, Indic _sahasrāra_. Two patterns, not one.
- **Reversion strengthens convergence 4.** "Access is by subtraction" was the weakest of the four claims. _Lianshen huanxu_ is a strong case for it: the end of the most elaborate anatomy in the field is the anatomy's disappearance.
- **Two bodies inside one tradition.** The medical/alchemical split in China parallels the contemplative/clinical split Gyatso found in Tibet. A pattern worth testing elsewhere: wherever a culture has both a healing profession and a cultivation lineage, do their bodies diverge?
- **The charts as art.** The _Neijing tu_ and _Xiuzhen tu_ are nineteenth- and eighteenth-century pictures of an inner landscape, made long after the doctrines they encode. For [[_Shinsei]] that is licence, not embarrassment: they are late artistic syntheses of a contemplative tradition, which is exactly the genre in question.
- **Terminology.** Use _dantian_, _jing_, _qi_, _shen_ and gloss them; do not write "Chinese subtle body." Consistent with the line already held in the parent note.
