# Personal History

I remember living in London, The busy attractive city that once symbolized the energy in my creative practice. Designer and art director by day working for the corporate world with clients such as Nike, Diesel. Activist and experimental artist by Night to express political and ethical views.

I guess it comes with the time and the identity one forms as one explore life and the creative energy,

I had conflicting ideas and mean to survive,
Glad to be aknoledged by the big brands and the designer circles! Feeling alive when expressing what I used to call the Frenetic feedback resonance and the flashing subliminal images in the underground.

It was a great time indeed until I joined the Shaolin temple in London. The time had come to further the meditation and Chin Gong. It only take a couple of months to feel the changes within.

It is when one practices the breath, develop an awareness for the vital energy that changes on a deeper level of the mind happens.

Cultivating mindfulness, Making the mind, the body, the energy one, we quickly realise that thoughts are only one aspects of being. Those thoughts are not ours, We attempt to navigate a world of information, doing our best to make sense of all the information that is presented to us. Especially as a designer and experimental artist. Those thoughts quickly turn limitless.

The results of this new awareness lead me towards a need to create a new life for myself. One that would be more ethical; A life that would celebrate a new sense of integrity. And it was clear that mindfulness and the exploration of consciousness would require a more constant form of meditation.

When mentioning meditation, we are often taught, to sit and breathe, to observe our thought, to develop an awareness for the feelings within us and an acute awareness of our surroundings. There are many techniques available to us, it is for each one of us to experiment with those meditative techniques that are offered to us.

Initially trained as a painter in the field of abstract painting, I will always remember my first class when joining the Royal Academy of Arts. Being 16 years old of age, it is a time in life when one develops a deeper sense of identity and attempts to become an adult. Some of us mature faster than others due to life circumstances. I can not say that the few years that followed were peaceful and without conflicts indeed. Many challenges presented themselves, including with the law and other situations we experience when left without supervision, street life as it is sometimes called. We quickly learn the codes and the dangers it represents.

What ever the political boundaries. There was something in abstract art that was fascinating, without realising it, I was actually practicing a form of meditation. It is one of the pleasures of painting as a medium, and especially as abstract art. I am not talking here about the deep masterful abstraction that is controlled, conceptualized to a point that it can become a science, but rather the pleasure of playing with paint, its texture, its light, the brush strokes. An exploration of consciousness. Especially at such an early stage when one has not form any ideas about the practice itself, when it is fresh and new and like a child we are in awe of the new feelings and discoveries we are experiencing for the first time.

I guess that paint brings memories of food, the first taste, holding the food in our hands, feeling the warmth, the coldness, the sweetness or acidity of the new flavours that creates chemical reactions and sensations within us.

It is with colours and textures that we explore a new language, The beauty of those is that as such an early stage, we have yet no words to describe those experiences. We are simply in a state of discovery.

Playing with matter, there are no mistakes or accidents as one allows for the flow to manifest. It is by remaining in a playful state of awareness that we let each element manifest its properties.

At the earlier stage of my academic journey, this abstract painting study was to lay the foundation for my practice until today. Considering the number of style, techniques and medium I have explored, it is true that at various stage the priorities are different. Sometimes we want to learn new techniques, for example, technical drawings, the quest for the perfect circle drawn with a computer software, or anatomic drawings might serve a different purpose on the artist journey.

When learning a new technique, the analytic mind might be necessary at its sharpest, but as soon as One master a technique, it is possible to get into the flow state again.

At the beginning, I used paint as a medium, what was the most fascinating was its weight and texture, even a few layers if not diluted, creates a surface that has its own life. The paint with its own weight, the time it takes to dry, the pleasure is in the discovery of the medium itself.

Years later in Japan, Following the path of a tattoo artist where waves are one of the most seen patterns, Japan, an Archipel of thousands of islands surrounded by water, the famous wave of Hokusai.

It is as if water replaced the numerous design and political symbols that used to occupy my mind while living in London. I remember following the twin towers, the intensity of the work I produced, a combination of war images, reflecting on the media and political propaganda, the western world was stuck between war and terrorism.

I much prefer the subject of water than the myriad of horror stories we witness on the media. Now that I mainly use ink and water, with a combination of mineral paints. That I use the brush or the needle, I am definitely dealing with a liquid. When working on the skin, one of the main challenges is not to let the lines blow as when unproper technique is applied. It is something it is hard to forgive yourself when you try to master lines even after years of practice.

Yet, sometimes we wish to keep that spontaneity, It is why I often come back to ink, water, liquid on paper. It is a fine balance between letting the water show you the path, playing with the brush, we know each brush as its own stroke. It is a balance of observation, playing at the edge of mastery.

Somehow, there are no mistakes but the mind that tells you it is not supposed to be the way it should. Yet by giving the time to the eyes and mind to refresh, by looking later at the experiment, it tells us that what was perceived as a mistake might have been an opportunity to grow.

It is somehow similar to meditation. When streaming a movie that might contain an agenda or propaganda, at first I want to switch it off and not waste my time, then I realise that the focus I have for example with ethics, inner peace are reaffirmed, So what ever is presented in front of my eyes, it reaffirms the real priorities that live within. As if we could remain in a constant state of meditation with the core values that compassion dictates.

We define the self yet forget the self. It is said that seld itself does not exists, but rather we are the result of a multitude of events that coincide to create our interpretation of reality which gives us a sense of self. But that sense of self would not have been the same, should we have lived a different life or experiencing a very different present.
