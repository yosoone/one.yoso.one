# How Buddhism Is Studied

## Summary

Academic Buddhist studies sorts its material along **three independent axes**: where and when a tradition lived (**region**), what kind of thing is being studied (**subject**), and which current of thought and practice it belongs to (**orientation**). The first two work well as shelves. The third does not: Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna run _across_ regions, so scholars treat them as currents rather than as places or sects.[^1]

This note is a learning map. It is not a plan to reorganise the vault, which keeps its current folders while the picture fills in.

---

## 1. Region and historical tradition

Introductions and reference works mostly divide Buddhism geographically and historically:[^2]

- **India** — the early teachings, the Mainstream schools and their Abhidharma, and the rise of Mahāyāna philosophy (Madhyamaka, Yogācāra) and of the tantras.
- **Theravāda** — Sri Lanka and mainland Southeast Asia.
- **East Asia** — China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam: Chan/Zen, Pure Land, Tiantai/Tendai, Huayan, Nichiren, and esoteric Buddhism (Shingon).
- **Tibet and the Himalaya** — Tibet, Bhutan, Mongolia, Nepal: the Nyingma, Sakya, Kagyu and Geluk schools.

## 2. Subject

Within or across regions, scholarship divides by the kind of evidence: **doctrine** (concepts, philosophy), **texts** (sūtras, commentaries, canons), **practice and ritual** (meditation, liturgy, death rites), **people** (teachers, lineages, communities), **history** (councils, transmission, politics) and **material culture** (art, architecture, objects).

## 3. Orientation: Mainstream, Mahāyāna, esoteric

These are the terms most often mistaken for places.

- **Mahāyāna** is not a single school or institution. Silk argues that it resists definition as an institution at all and is better understood as a loose family of texts, ideals and practices found across many regions.[^3]
- **Esoteric (tantric) Buddhism** likewise spans India, Tibet and East Asia; recent scholarship studies Chinese, Korean and Japanese esoteric traditions, Shingon included, alongside the Tibetan.[^4]

### The vehicles

Tibetan scholastics place tantra _inside_ the Mahāyāna. The Great Vehicle has two paths: the **Perfection Vehicle** (_pāramitāyāna_), the sūtra path of the six perfections, and the **Mantra Vehicle** (_mantrayāna_, Vajrayāna), the tantric path. Both aim at buddhahood for the sake of all beings; they are said to differ in method and speed, not in view.[^5]

The popular triad of **Hīnayāna, Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna** as three separate vehicles is a later simplification. "Hīnayāna" ("lesser vehicle") is a Mahāyāna polemical term, and scholars now prefer "Mainstream" or "Nikāya" Buddhism for the non-Mahāyāna schools.[^6]

### "Tibetan" is not the same as "Vajrayāna"

- **Tibetan Buddhism is more than Vajrayāna.** It includes sūtra Mahāyāna, monastic discipline, logic and debate, and popular religion. Kamalaśīla's sūtra-based _Bhāvanākrama_ stands at its foundation (see [[Samye Debate - Method and Immediacy|Samye Debate]]); the windhorse of [[Lungta]] belongs to popular religion with pre-Buddhist roots.[^7]
- **Vajrayāna is more than Tibetan.** Japanese Shingon is esoteric Mahāyāna too (see [[Shingon and Vajrayana]]).

### An example: the intermediate state

The idea of a state between death and rebirth (_antarābhava_) is older than tantra. It was debated among the Indian Mainstream schools: accepted by the Sarvāstivādins and described by Vasubandhu, rejected by the Theravādins.[^8] In East Asia it underlies the seven-week cycle of memorial rites, as in Japan's forty-ninth-day rite (四十九日, _shijūkunichi_).[^9] The Tibetan **six bardos**, and the _Bardo Thödol_ built on them, are the tantric elaboration of that older idea.[^10] One concept, three regions, two orientations — which is why [[Bardo]] sits with Vajrayāna in the vault while the wider idea would belong with doctrine.

## 4. How the vault relates

The vault's folders currently mix the axes: `Esoteric` is an orientation, `Zen` and `Japan` are school and region, `Concepts`, `Sutras`, `Teachers` and `Practice` are subjects. That is workable. If the vault is ever reorganised on academic lines, the usual rule would be **folders for region and subject; orientation as a property** (e.g. `tradition:` and `vehicle:`), so that one query can gather, say, every esoteric note from both Tibet and Japan.

A possible shape, for later:

```
Buddhism/
├── Doctrine/              (today's Concepts)
├── Traditions/
│   ├── India/
│   ├── East Asia/         Zen · Shingon · Pure Land
│   └── Tibet and Himalaya/
├── Texts/                 (today's Sutras)
├── People/                (today's Teachers)
├── History/
├── Practice/
└── Comparative and Science/
```
