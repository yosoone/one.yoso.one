## Orientation

Buddhism is perceived as a religion. Today, however, it can also be described as a path and a series of methods — fundamentally, a path and a set of techniques for studying life.

Tested for over 2,500 years by people at every level: the educated and the blindly devoted alike. A systematic analysis of what we call the mind, developed alongside Taoism and the other Indian traditions.

The essence, as the Dalai Lama frames it, is [[Compassion]] — root and cause. From there: the cause of suffering, and the Eightfold Path.[^1]

The technology of the printing press, as it developed in China, produced what is generally held to be the world's earliest dated printed book — a copy of the [[Diamond Sutra|Diamond Sutra]].[^2]

For how the field itself is organised — regions, subjects, and Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna as currents rather than places — see [[How Buddhism Is Studied]].

## Key passages

### Emptiness and compassion

> The crucial thing is to combine <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">[[Compassion]] for others with an understanding that persons and phenomena are empty of inherent existence.</mark> I've put these two principles into effect and they have brought me great benefit. Things appear to have some sort of essential existence, but it is only an appearance.[^3]

As with a chicken soup: it is an illusion in the mind — there is no such thing as _a chicken soup_.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Being empty of self is different from not existing; it simply means that, from the point of view of experience, that which comes into existence does so from non-existence, and subsequently returns to non-existence; it has no existence outside of the single moment.</mark>[^4]

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Conclusion: live in the moment. Focus on kindness and compassion, and let it unfold.</mark>

### Ingesting suffering

> We need to recognize and identify the spiritual and material foods we have ingested that are causing us to suffer.[^5]

Understanding [[Suffering]] — and solving suffering.
