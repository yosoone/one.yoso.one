# Lungta (རླུང་རྟ) — The Windhorse

![[Other/Buddhism/Concepts/Lungta Diagram.svg|991]]

## Overview

_Lungta_ (Tibetan: རླུང་རྟ, Wylie: _rlung rta_) combines _lung_ (རླུང), wind or air — one of the five elements — with _ta_ (རྟ), horse.[^1] The term names both the mythical creature depicted at the center of Tibetan prayer flags and the flags themselves, which are also called _darchog_.[^2]

The windhorse predates Buddhism in Tibet, originating in the indigenous Bon tradition before being absorbed into Tibetan Buddhist practice from the seventh and eighth centuries onward — one of the clearest examples of Bon and Buddhist symbolism blending rather than one simply replacing the other.[^3]

## The Composition

A standard Lungta flag is built around a fixed layout:

- **Center** — the windhorse itself, galloping through clouds and carrying a flaming wish-fulfilling jewel on its back. The jewel represents the Three Jewels of Buddhism: the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.[^4]
- **Surrounding field** — mantras and prayers in Tibetan script, filling the space around the central image.[^1]
- **Four corners** — the Four Dignities (also called the Four Powerful Ones or Four Mythical Creatures), arranged clockwise from the upper left: Garuda, Dragon, Snow Lion, Tiger.[^5] Together they represent qualities cultivated on the bodhisattva path — fearlessness (Garuda), gentle power (Dragon), clear awareness (Snow Lion), and confidence (Tiger).[^5]

## The Five Elements and Colour Order

Strung sets of flags (_darchog_) always follow the same left-to-right colour sequence, corresponding to the five elements of Tibetan cosmology and medicine:

| Colour | Element |
|---|---|
| Blue | Space |
| White | Air / Wind |
| Red | Fire |
| Green | Water |
| Yellow | Earth |

Each person's own _lungta_ — their vital energy or life-force — is also associated with one of the five elements according to their birth year.[^6]

## Levels of Meaning

Lungta can be read on several levels at once:

- **Outer** — a mythical creature combining the speed of wind and the strength of a horse, carrying prayers from earth to the heavens.[^2]
- **Inner** — the idea that the mind rides upon the body's subtle energy or inner wind (_prana_); when this energy is strong and harmonised, the mind finds it easier to rest in its true nature.[^7]
- **Secret** — the elemental and energetic dimension in which lungta refers directly to the movement of _prana_ within the subtle body, and the mind itself as that which the windhorse carries.[^7]

The flags are not meant to carry a message to a deity. Instead, wind is understood to "read" the printed prayers and disperse their vibration across the landscape; as a flag fades and frays, its blessing is understood to pass fully into the world around it.[^2]
