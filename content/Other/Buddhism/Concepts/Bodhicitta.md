# Bodhicitta

In [Mahayana Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana), **bodhicitta**\[a] ("aspiration to [enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism)" or "the thought of awakening") is the mind ([citta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitta_\(Buddhism\) "Chitta (Buddhism)")) that is aimed at awakening ([bodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism)) through [wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prajnaparamita) and [compassion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karu%E1%B9%87%C4%81) for the benefit of all [sentient beings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentient_beings_\(Buddhism\) "Sentient beings (Buddhism)").\[1]\[2]\[3]

Bodhicitta is the defining quality of the Mahayana [bodhisattva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva) (a being striving towards [Buddhahood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhahood)) and the act of giving rise to bodhicitta (_bodhicittotpāda)_ is what makes a bodhisattva a bodhisattva. Bodhicitta is the generative cause of a bodhisattva's eventual Buddhahood.\[3] The _[Daśabhūmika Sūtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Stages_Sutra)_ explains that the arising of bodhicitta is the first step in the bodhisattva's career.\[4]

## Etymology

Etymologically, the word is a combination of the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) words [bodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi) and [citta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citta). _Bodhi_ means "awakening" or "enlightenment". _Citta_ derives from the Sanskrit root _cit_, and means "that which is conscious" (i.e., mind or consciousness). \_Bodhicitta\_may be translated as "awakening mind" or "mind of enlightenment".\[5] It is also sometimes translated as "the thought of enlightenment."\[6]

## Definition

### Indian sources

The term bodhicitta is defined and explained in different ways by different [Mahayana Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) sources. According to Paul Williams, the basic meaning of bodhicitta in Indian sources (such as [Atisha's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ati%C5%9Ba) _[Bodhipathapradipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhipathapradipa)_) is the lofty motivation to "strive to bring a complete end to all the sufferings of others along with their own suffering...This bodhicitta results from deep compassion (_karuna_) for the suffering of others."\[7]

According to the [_Bodhisattvabhumi_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogacarabhumi-sastra), the bodhisattva who gives rise to bodhicitta thinks thus:

> O may I obtain supreme and perfect Enlightenment, promote the good of all beings, and establish them in the final and complete nirvana and in the Buddha-knowledge!\[8]

Thus, according to the _Bodhisattvabhumi_, bodhicitta has two objects of thought or themes (_[alambana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80lambana)_): _bodhi_ and the good of the living beings (_sattv-ārtha_).\[8]

Similarly, in the Ornament of Realization ([_Abhisamayālaṁkāra_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhisamayalankara)), bodhicitta is defined as follows:\[9]

> The arising of the mind \[of awakening] is a desire for perfect, complete Bodhi, for the sake of others (Skt. cittotpādaḥ parārthāya samyaksambodhikāmatā)

According to Indian sources, the bodhicitta aspiration provides incalculable [merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merit_\(Buddhism\) "Merit (Buddhism)")(such as good [rebirths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebirth_\(Buddhism\) "Rebirth (Buddhism)"), a weakening of the [defilements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleshas_\(Buddhism\) "Kleshas (Buddhism)"), increased mindfulness and luck).\[10] Bodhicitta is what makes someone a Mahayana bodhisattva, a child of the Buddha. Thus, the Indian Buddhist author [Shantideva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantideva) (8th century) writes in his _[Bodhicaryavatara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattvacary%C4%81vat%C4%81ra)_:

> Those who long to transcend the hundreds of miseries of existence, who long to relieve creatures of their sorrows, who long to enjoy many hundreds of joys, must never abandon bodhicitta. When bodhicitta has arisen in him, a wretch, captive in the prison of existence, he is straightway hailed son of the Sugatas \[the Buddhas], to be revered in the worlds of gods and men.\[11]

According to [Paul Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Williams_\(Buddhist_studies_scholar\) "Paul Williams (Buddhist studies scholar)"), _bodhicitta_ in early Mahāyāna works was less well defined and meant a "certain state of mind" characteristic of a bodhisattva.\[12]

The cultivation of bodhicitta plays a significant role in Mahāyāna liturgies, particularly in ceremonies where one takes the [bodhisattva precepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva_Precepts). Within these texts, two distinct types of bodhicitta are identified. The first, known as "conventional bodhicitta" (samvrti), refers to the mental aspiration of a bodhisattva to attain enlightenment, as previously explained. The second, called "ultimate bodhicitta" ([paramartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Param%C4%81rtha-satya)), denotes a mind that directly perceives either emptiness or the inherent enlightenment / [buddha-nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha-nature) within the mind itself.\[3]

Regarding the second aspect, some Mahayana sources discuss bodhicitta not as something that must be generated, but as something which is latent in all beings, something that is already there.\[3] This is linked to the idea of the inherently pure [luminous mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminous_mind) (prakrtipariśuddhacitta). Thus, the \_[Vairocanābhisaṃbodhi-sūtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vairocan%C4%81bhisa%E1%B9%83bodhi_S%C5%ABtra)\_states: “Knowing one’s own mind according to reality is BODHI, and bodhicitta is the innately pure mind that is originally existent.”\[3]

### Modern definitions

According to the [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama), bodhicitta is:

> the aspiration to bring about the welfare of all sentient beings and to attain buddhahood for their sake - is really the distilled essence, the squeeze juice, of all the Buddha's teachings, because ultimately, the Buddha's intention is to lead all sentient beings to perfect enlightenment.\[13]

Some modern East Asian authors on Buddhism, such as [D.T. Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._T._Suzuki) and [M. Anesaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaharu_Anesaki), define bodhicitta as an immanent inner awakening. For example, Anesaki writes that bodhicitta is "the primordial essence of our mind, which in itself consists in the supreme bodhi."\[2]

According to [Zoketsu Norman Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoketsu_Norman_Fischer), bodhicitta is a spontaneous wish to attain enlightenment motivated by great compassion for all sentient beings, accompanied by a falling away of the attachment to the illusion of an inherently existing self.\[14]

Fischer adds that bodhicitta, along with the mind of great compassion (_mahakaruna_), motivates one to attain enlightenment [Buddhahood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhahood), as quickly as possible and benefit infinite sentient beings through their emanations and other skillful means. Bodhicitta is a felt need to replace others' suffering with bliss. Since the ultimate end of suffering is [nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana), bodhicitta necessarily involves a motivation to help others to awaken (to find _[bodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi)_).\[14]

## Types and stages of bodhicitta

### Indian sources

Indian Mahayana sources also developed different models which described different forms and levels of bodhicitta.\[15]

According to the _[Bodhisattvabhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yog%C4%81c%C4%81rabh%C5%ABmi-%C5%9A%C4%81stra),_ there are two main stages of the development of bodhicitta:\[15]

- a kind of bodhicitta which can be lost
- the permanent kind of bodhicitta which cannot be lost and leads directly to enlightenment.

Furthermore, according to [Shantideva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantideva) (and other sources), there are two types of bodhicitta from a practical perspective:\[15]\[3]

- bodhicitta which is a wish or aspiration (Sanskrit: _bodhipranidhicitta_ or _pranidhicittotpada_), this is when a bodhisattva makes an aspiration or "bodhisattva vow" ([bodhisattva praṇidhāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva_vow)) to become a Buddha for the sake of all beings. This is compared to making the decision to start on a journey.
- active bodhicitta (_bodhiprasthanacitta_ or _prasthanacittotpada_) which consists of actually practicing the path in line with one's intent, which like actually undertaking a journey

According to the _[Buddhāvataṃsaka Sūtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddh%C4%81vata%E1%B9%83saka_S%C5%ABtra),_ there are also said to be three types of bodhicitta:\[3]

- herder bodhicitta, in which a bodhisattva strives to deliver beings to awakening first (like a herder lets all their sheep in the pen first)
- ferryman bodhicitta, in which they travel to and enter enlightenment together with the beings they are ferrying
- king-like bodhicitta, in which the bodhisattva reaches awakening first, and then helps others to reach that goal

### In Tibetan Buddhism

A common Tibetan Buddhist distinction is that between relative and absolute (or ultimate) bodhicitta.\[16] _Relative bodhicitta_ is a state of mind in which the practitioner works for the good of all beings as if it were their own.\[16] _Absolute bodhicitta_ is the wisdom of [shunyata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunyata)\[16] (śunyatā, a Sanskrit term often translated as "emptiness", though the alternatives "vast expanse" or "openness" or "spaciousness" probably convey the idea better to Westerners).\[17]

In his book _Words of My Perfect Teacher_, the [Tibetan Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhist) teacher [Patrul Rinpoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrul_Rinpoche) describes the three degrees of bodhicitta as taught in the Avatamsaka Sutra, claiming that shepherd-like is best.\[18]

Some bodhicitta practices emphasize the absolute (e.g. [vipaśyanā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vipassana)), while others emphasize the relative (e.g. [metta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitr%C4%AB)), but both aspects are seen in all Mahāyāna practice as essential to enlightenment, especially in the [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet) practices of [tonglen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonglen)\[19] and [lojong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lojong).\[14] Without the absolute, the relative can degenerate into pity and sentimentality, whereas the absolute without the relative can lead to nihilism and lack of desire to engage other sentient beings for their benefit.

## Cultivation

[Mahāyāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81y%C4%81na) Buddhist practice focuses on the [Bodhisattva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva) path, at this path begins with the arousing of bodhicitta.\[20]\[21] The arising of bodhicitta or the generation of it (as well as the mind generated in this process) is called _bodhicittotpāda_ (Sanskrit; Chinese: 發菩提心, faputixin; Jp. hotsubodaishin; Tibetan: byang chub kyi sems bskyed pa), that is, the arising (_utpāda_) of bodhicitta.\[21]

Mahāyāna Buddhism teaches that the broader motivation of achieving one's own enlightenment "in order to help all sentient beings" is the best possible motivation one can have for any action, whether it be working in one's vocation, teaching others, or even making an incense offering. Thus, the bodhisattva's Six Perfections ([Pāramitās](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81ramit%C4%81)) of Buddhism only become true "perfections" when they are done with the motivation of bodhicitta. This means that any act, such as the action of giving (Skt. [dāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%81na)) can be done in a mundane sense, or it can be a [pāramitā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81ramit%C4%81) if it is conjoined with bodhicitta. Bodhicitta is the primary positive factor to be cultivated.

### Practices

The Mahāyāna tradition provides specific methods for the intentional cultivation of both absolute and relative bodhicitta.\[22] This cultivation is considered to be a fundamental aspect of the path to [Buddhahood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhahood). <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">Practitioners of the Mahāyāna make it their primary goal to develop genuine uncontrived bodhicitta, which remains within their mindstreams continuously without having to rely on conscious effort</mark>. This is assisted by numerous methods, contemplation, rituals and meditations, such as: relying on a [spiritual friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaly%C4%81%E1%B9%87a-mittat%C4%81), taking refuge in the [three jewels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refuge_in_Buddhism), and contemplating the defects of [samsara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%E1%B9%83s%C4%81ra) (cyclic existence), the benefits of arousing bodhicitta (as well as the downsides of abandoning it), and developing spiritual qualities such as faith ([sraddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Ar%C4%81ddha_\(Buddhadharma\) "Śrāddha (Buddhadharma)")), mindfulness and wisdom ([prajña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praj%C3%B1%C4%81_\(Buddhism\) "Prajñā (Buddhism)")).\[23]

Numerous Mahāyāna texts outline various causes and conditions that foster the development of _bodhicitta_. The _[Bodhisattvabhūmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogachara)_ identifies four primary conditions (_adhipatipratyaya_) for generating _bodhicitta_:\[21]

1. Witnessing an extraordinary miracle (_[ṛddhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%B9%9Addhi)-prātihārya_) performed by a buddha or bodhisattva,
2. Hearing teachings on enlightenment (_[bodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi)_) or doctrines directed at bodhisattvas (_bodhisattva-piṭaka_),
3. Recognizing the potential [extinction of the dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Dharma) and striving to preserve the true dharma (_saddharma_),
4. Perceiving the [suffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%E1%B8%A5kha) caused by afflictions (_[kleśa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleshas_\(Buddhism\) "Kleshas (Buddhism)")_) in sentient beings and feeling compassion for them.

Another list is provided in [Vasubandhu's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasubandhu) _Treatise on the Bodhisattva Vow_ (_Fa putixinjing lun_):\[21]

1. Contemplating the buddhas,
2. Contemplating the inherent dangers (_ādīnava_) of the body,
3. Cultivating compassion (_karuṇā_) for sentient beings,
4. Aspiring for the supreme result or fruit (_phala_), i.e. Buddhahood

According to Ulrich Pagel, numerous Mahāyāna sūtras, like the _[Bodhisattvapiṭaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bodhisattvapi%E1%B9%ADaka\&action=edit\&redlink=1),_see the arising of _bodhicitta_ (_bodhicittotpāda_) as an ongoing process which must be constantly refurbished (rather than as a static event which only happens once).\[12] At the outset of his _[Madhyamakāvatāra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhyamak%C4%81vat%C4%81ra)_, [Candrakīrti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrakirti) likens compassion (_[karuṇā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karu%E1%B9%87%C4%81)_) to a seed, water, and crops, emphasizing its significance at every stage of the bodhisattva's journey. Compassion is crucial at the beginning, as it initiates the bodhisattva's path; in the middle, as it sustains the practitioner and prevents regression into the limited nirvāṇa of an arhat; and at the end, where it manifests as the ceaseless, spontaneous actions of a fully enlightened being for the benefit of others. _Karuṇā_ is thus regarded as the root of _bodhicitta_ because the arising and enduring presence of _bodhicitta_ depend on the strength of _mahākaruṇā_, or profound empathy for the suffering of all beings.\[3] To cultivate bodhicitta then, it is important to cultivate compassion.

A common practice in various [Mahāyāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81y%C4%81na) Buddhist traditions is to recite [bodhisattva vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva_vow) and aspiration prayers or chants which help give rise to bodhicitta. One popular chant in India and presently throughout the Mahayana world is the _Bhadracaripraṇidhāna_ (_Vows of Good Conduct_) or _Ārya-samantabhadra-caryā-praṇidhāna-rāja_ (_The Royal Vow to follow the Noble Course of Conduct of Samantabhadra_), a verse aspiration prayer which appears at the end of some versions of the [_Avatamsaka sutra_.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddh%C4%81vata%E1%B9%83saka_S%C5%ABtra)\[24] This text, originally an independent set of verses, is cited in numerous sources and was known to figures like [Bhavya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bh%C4%81viveka), [Śantideva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantideva), and [Kamalaśīla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala%C5%9B%C4%ABla).\[24]

One short prayer for bodhicitta which is very popular in Tibetan Buddhism was composed by the Indian paṇḍita Dīpaṁkaraśrījñāna ([Atiśa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ati%C5%9Ba)):\[9]

> In Sanskrit:\
> buddhaṁ ca dharmaṁ ca gaṇottamaṁ ca \
> yāvad dhi bodhiṁ śaraṇaṁ prayāmi\
> dānādikr̥ tyaiś ca kr̥ tair mayaibhir \
> buddho bhaveyaṁ jagato hitāya

> In the Buddha, the Dharma,\
> and the Best among Assemblies,\
> I go for refuge until awakening;\
> by the good deeds of giving, etc.,\
> performed by me,\
> may I become a Buddha

### East Asian Buddhism

According to the _[Awakening of Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awakening_of_Faith_in_the_Mahayana)_, a key treatise in [East Asian Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Buddhism), there are three forms of arising of bodhicitta: arising from faith, arising from understanding and practice, and arising from realization.\[21]

[Jingying Huiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingying_Huiyuan) (523–592), in his _Dasheng Yizhang_ (Compendium on the Purport of Mahāyāna), categorizes the generation of bodhicitta into three types:\[21]

1. generation of the mind based on characteristics, where the bodhisattva, seeing the nature of _saṃsāra_ and _nirvāṇa_, develops aversion to _saṃsāra_ and strives for _nirvāṇa_;
2. generation of the mind beyond characteristics, where the bodhisattva understands the [non-duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondualism) between _saṃsāra_ and _nirvāṇa_ and transcends the perception of their distinct qualities;
3. generation of the mind based on truth, where the bodhisattva recognizes the original nature of awakening as identical to their own mind and returns to their [original mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_enlightenment).

In East Asian traditions, a common method of developing bodhicitta is by reciting Sramana [Zhiyi's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhiyi) four-fold [bodhisattva vow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva_vows) formula, which is:\[25]

> Sentient beings are numberless, I vow to save them all; \
> Afflictions are inexhaustible, I vow to end them all; \
> Dharma doors are boundless, I vow to master them all; \
> Buddhahood is unsurpassable, I vow to attain it.

There is also a bodhicitta [mantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantra) which is recited in some traditions of [Chinese esoteric Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Esoteric_Buddhism) and [Shingon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shingon_Buddhism). The Sanskrit mantra is:

> Oṃ Bodhicittam Utpādayāmi (Om I aspire to develop the Awakened mind).\[26]

### Tibetan methods

Among the many methods for developing uncontrived bodhicitta given in Tibetan Mahāyāna teachings are:

- A. So as to arouse Bodhicitta, the main aspect, the Four Immeasurables ([Brahmavihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmavihara)) contemplation and practice:
  - Immeasurable Loving-Kindness ([Maitrī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitr%C4%AB)),
  - Immeasurable Compassion ([Karunā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karu%E1%B9%87%C4%81)),
  - Immeasurable Joy in the Good Fortune of Others ([Muditā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudit%C4%81)),
  - Immeasurable Equanimity ([Upekṣā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upeksa)) and
- B. So as to aspire Bodhicitta:
  - The [Lojong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lojong) (_mind training_) practices:
    - Others as equal to self: Exchanging self and others: ([Tonglen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonglen)) the Sending and Receiving while breathing practice,
    - Others as more important: Viewing all other sentient beings as having been our mothers in infinite past lives, and feeling gratitude for the many occasions on which they have taken care of us.
- C. So as to apply Bodhicitta and achieve enlightenment:
  - The repeated Pāramitās practice cycle: [1) Generosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%81na), [2) Virtue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9A%C4%ABla), [3) Patience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%E1%B9%A3%C4%81nti), [4) Effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C4%ABrya), [5) Meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhy%C4%81na_in_Buddhism), and [6) Insight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praj%C3%B1%C4%81_\(Buddhism\) "Prajñā (Buddhism)").

Some Tibetan Buddhist lineages, particularly within the Nyingma tradition, incorporate [Dream Yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Yoga) (_milam_) as a method for cultivating bodhicitta. This practice involves maintaining awareness during dreams to simultaneously develop compassion for dream beings and recognize the illusory nature of all phenomena, thereby training both relative and absolute bodhicitta. \[27]

In [Lojong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lojong)'s 59 slogans, Point Two: The main practice, which is training in absolute and relative bodhicitta.

A. _Absolute Bodhicitta_

Slogan 2. Regard all dharmas as dreams; although experiences may seem solid, they are passing memories.

Slogan 3. Examine the nature of unborn awareness.

Slogan 4. Self-liberate even the antidote.

Slogan 5. Rest in the nature of [alaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaya), the essence, the present moment.

Slogan 6. In post-meditation, be a child of illusion.

B._Relative Bodhicitta_

Slogan 7. Sending and taking should be practiced alternately. These two should ride the breath (aka. practice [Tonglen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonglen)).

Slogan 8. Three objects, [three poisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_poisons_\(Buddhism\) "Three poisons (Buddhism)"), three roots of virtue—the 3 objects are friends, enemies and neutrals. The 3 poisons are craving, aversion and indifference. The 3 roots of virtue are the remedies.

Slogan 9. In all activities, train with slogans.

Slogan 10. Begin the sequence of sending and taking with yourself.

When only realizing [Śūnyatā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emptiness), the practitioner might not benefit others, so the Mahayana path unites emptiness and compassion, this keeps from falling into the two limits and remaining on the middle way. Traditionally, Bodhisattvas practice meditative concentration at the beginning toward attaining the noble one's wisdom level, then the main practice becomes benefiting others spontaneously, unlike other paths that might discontinue benefiting others.

All the conducive causes and auspicious conditions should be complete for bodhicitta to properly arise. After continued training, these qualities can arise in the mind without contrivance.

The two main traditions in taking the Bodhicitta vows are: 1) [Nagarjuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarjuna)'s profound view chariot and, 2) [Asanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asanga)'s vast conduct chariot. After which this is guarded with what to avoid, and what to adopt.

The practice can be divided into three parts: 1) mind training, 2) arousing bodhicitta, and 3) training in what to adopt and what to avoid. These can be called the 1) preliminary practice, 2) main practice, and 3) concluding practice. The preliminary practice is training in the four boundless qualities. The main practice is arousing Bodhicitta and taking vows. The concluding practice is training in what to adopt and guarding without fail against what to avoid.\[28]

The [Ancient Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyingma) school [preliminary practice cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C3%B6ndro) in the [Samantabhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi-Buddha) to [Longchenpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longchenpa) to [Jigme Lingpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jigme_Lingpa)'s lineage of the [Excellent Part to Omniscience: Vast Expanse Heart Essence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longchen_Nyingthig). Invocation; Confession; [Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_in_Nyingma_Buddhist_Dharma) with [Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refuge_\(Buddhism\) "Refuge (Buddhism)"): [Mind Series Bodhichitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzogchen) nature in the [channels, inner air, and tigles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtle_body#Buddhism); Mandala of essence, nature, and compassion; Generation: Illusory perceptions like the moon reflecting in the water. Follow like [Manjushree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjushree) to dedicate with the aspiration to realize the innermost meaning and realize to attain [Buddhahood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhahood) as a [spiritual warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_warrior).\[29]

#### Two practice lineages

Tibetan Buddhists maintain that there are two main ways to cultivate Bodhichitta, the "Seven Causes and Effects" that originates from Maitreya and was taught by Atisha, and "Exchanging Self and Others," taught by Shantideva and originally by Manjushri.

According to [Tsongkhapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je_Tsongkhapa) the seven causes and effects are thus:

1. recognizing all beings as your mothers;
2. recollecting their kindness;
3. the wish to repay their kindness;
4. love;
5. great compassion;
6. wholehearted resolve;
7. bodhichitta.

According to [Pabongka Rinpoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabongkhapa_D%C3%A9chen_Nyingpo) the second method consists of the following meditations:\[30]\[31]

1. how self and others are equal;
2. contemplating the many faults resulting from self-cherishing;
3. contemplating the many good qualities resulting from cherishing others;
4. the actual contemplation on the interchange of self and others;
5. with these serving as the basis, the way to meditate on giving and taking ([tonglen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonglen)).

### Universality

The practice and realization of bodhicitta are independent of sectarian considerations, since they are fundamentally a part of the human experience. Bodhisattvas are not only recognized in the Theravāda school of Buddhism,\[32] but in all other religious traditions and among those of no formal religious tradition.\[33]The present fourteenth [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama), for instance, regarded [Mother Teresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa) as one of the greatest modern bodhisattvas.\[34]
