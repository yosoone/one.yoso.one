# Dopamine and Dukkha: The Player's Experience in _Battlefield V_ and _Battlefield 6_

## Two objects of analysis

_Battlefield V_ (DICE/EA, 2018) and _Battlefield 6_ (Battlefield Studios/EA, 2025) share a core loop: short rounds, frequent death, respawn, repeat. They differ sharply in what is built on top of it, which makes the pair informative.

_Battlefield V_ shipped after the _Star Wars Battlefront II_ backlash with no premium pass and no randomised loot crates. Company Coin was earned only by playing; Battlefield Currency was bought with money and spent only on cosmetics. Progression ran across five rank ladders with daily missions and time-limited "Tides of War" chapters.[^1]

_Battlefield 6_ restores a full live-service economy and elaborates the ladder enormously. Three XP currencies run at once: Career XP, Hardware XP (tracked separately for every individual weapon and vehicle), and Battle Pass XP, earned one-to-one with Career XP. A functional hardware unlock is granted at _every Career Rank from 2 to 50_; cosmetics then arrive every two ranks to 100; beyond that ranks continue "into the thousands," which EA describes plainly as being for pride. Weapons and vehicles rank independently, unlocking attachments, packages and camouflages, and generating badges at Hardware Ranks 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 — 228 badges at launch, "more with each Season." Seasonal Battle Passes run 100+ tiers across four branching paths plus Ultimate and Prestige tracks "for completionists," and expire at season's end.[^2] [^3]

The useful point is this. _Battlefield V_ removed the gambling layer and the compulsive pull remained, so the pull is not a slot machine bolted to a shooter. It is the firefight — an uncertain reward on an unpredictable schedule. _Battlefield 6_ keeps that engine and adds a second one that runs on certainty rather than uncertainty. The two map onto different links in the Buddhist chain, and conflating them is the standard error in writing on games and craving.

## Engine one: the firefight

### What dopamine actually signals

The popular account — games "flood the brain with dopamine," dopamine is pleasure — is wrong in a way that matters for any contemplative reading.

Games do release striatal dopamine. Koepp and colleagues showed it in 1998 using raclopride displacement and PET: binding fell during play, most strongly in the ventral striatum, and the effect tracked performance.[^4] But dopamine is not the pleasure. Schultz, Dayan and Montague established that midbrain dopamine neurons encode _reward prediction error_ — the gap between expectation and outcome. Better than expected, a burst; worse, a dip.[^5] It is a teaching signal about surprise, not a hedonic reading.

Berridge and Robinson went further: dopamine mediates "wanting" (incentive salience), not "liking" (hedonic impact), and the two come apart. Wanting can be amplified far beyond any pleasure the outcome delivers, persist against sincere intention to stop, and be triggered by cues rather than by the reward itself.[^6]

That is the neuroscientific statement of something the Pali sources say structurally: the force that pulls is not the satisfaction that arrives, and it can outlive it entirely.

### What the shooter evidence shows

The most relevant study is Mathiak and colleagues' fMRI work on players inside a first-person shooter, and its findings are counterintuitive enough to reorganise the analysis.[^7]

Killing an opponent — the scored, rewarded, ostensibly desired event — produced no activation of reward structures; the caudate showed, if anything, a trend toward deactivation even on success. What produced a large, reliable signal was _being killed_: bilateral caudate and orbitofrontal deactivation, the signature of a negative reward prediction error. The authors conclude that players continue in order to avoid the experience of failure. Right temporal pole activity during failure predicted whose mood survived the session, and group positive affect measured before and after play significantly _decreased_.

The round-to-round loop is therefore not powered by the pleasure of virtual killing. It is powered by the aversive drop when expectation collapses, and the next round is the move that avoids that drop.

### Reading the loop as paṭiccasamuppāda

The Buddhist sequence runs contact → feeling-tone (_vedanā_) → craving (_taṇhā_) → clinging (_upādāna_) → becoming. Judson Brewer has made the mapping to reward-based learning explicit and empirical: trigger–behaviour–result is operant conditioning restating dependent origination, and the hinge in both is feeling-tone, the pleasant/unpleasant/neutral valence assigned before any elaborated emotion.[^8] His clinical work turns the mapping into a mechanism, targeting posterior cingulate reactivity through awareness of craving rather than suppression of it.[^9]

Placed in that frame, the Mathiak result yields a correction. The firefight loop is not chiefly _kāma-taṇhā_, craving for sense pleasure. It runs on _dosa_ — aversion, the push against unpleasant feeling-tone. The death screen is the trigger; queueing again is the behaviour; the removal of that feeling is the "reward." Negative reinforcement in the technical sense, a closed causal loop in the doctrinal one.

The three roots cover this exactly. Attachment, aversion and confusion are not three flavours of one pull; aversion is a distinct engine, and it is the one running here.

### The leper at the fire pit

The sharpest canonical image for a negative-reinforcement loop is in the Māgaṇḍiya Sutta. A man with sores scratches at them and holds his body over glowing coals. The heat gives relief; the wounds worsen; the more he burns himself the more he must. The diagnosis is not that the relief is imaginary — it is real, and it is _measured against the itch_. The term for the mistake is distorted perception (_viparīta-saññā_): what is actually painful registers as pleasant, because the faculties of someone consumed by craving are impaired.[^10]

That is a precise description of a reward system calibrated to prediction error rather than to pleasure. Relief from a self-maintained deficit reads, from the inside, as enjoyment.

The same discourse supplies the method, and it is not moralising. Experience is examined under three heads: _assāda_ (gratification), _ādīnava_ (drawback), _nissaraṇa_ (escape). The gratification is granted first. Any Buddhist account of gaming that skips to the drawback has abandoned the tradition's own procedure.

### The suffering built into change

Koob and Le Moal's model of addiction as hedonic homeostatic dysregulation adds the temporal dimension.[^11] Repeated stimulation of reward circuitry recruits opposing, "antireward" processes; the baseline shifts; the same stimulus returns less and its absence costs more.[^12] The system does not merely fail to deliver — it moves the floor.

This is _vipariṇāma-dukkha_, the unsatisfactoriness belonging to change rather than to pain: pleasant states are unsatisfactory precisely because they are conditioned and must alter.[^13] The flat, irritable hour after a long session is not an accident of the evening. It is the opponent process arriving on schedule, and the tradition predicted its shape without knowing its chemistry.

## Engine two: the unlock ladder

The firefight is uncertain. The ladder is not. Different reinforcement schedule, and apparently a different dopamine signal.

### A near-continuous schedule over a variable one

Ferster and Skinner's taxonomy separates variable-ratio reinforcement — unpredictable, extinction-resistant, productive of high steady rates — from schedules where the payout is certain and counted.[^14] _Battlefield 6_ runs both at once. Each engagement is variable-ratio. Each unit of XP is not: it accrues reliably, displays continuously, and pays out on a published table. A player who loses every firefight for an hour still advances.

That redundancy is the design's real strength. When the uncertain engine gives nothing, the certain one keeps paying. Failure no longer terminates reinforcement; it only slows it.

### Ramping, not spiking

Prediction-error bursts are phasic and brief, poorly suited to sustaining effort toward a distant goal. Howe, Tierney, Sandberg, Phillips and Graybiel identified a second mode: striatal dopamine that _ramps up gradually_ as an animal approaches a remote reward, scaling with both proximity and size, plausibly functioning as sustained motivational drive rather than as a teaching signal.[^15]

The XP bar is that ramp given a user interface. It renders proximity to a distant reward continuously visible, which is exactly the variable the ramping signal tracks.

### The goal gradient, and progress handed to you

The behavioural literature agrees. The goal-gradient effect — effort rises as the goal nears — was demonstrated in rats by Hull and revived for human consumers by Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng, who found café loyalty-card holders buying at shortening intervals as the card filled. They also showed that the _illusion_ of progress works: a twelve-stamp card with two stamps pre-applied is completed faster than a plain ten-stamp card requiring identical purchases.[^16] [^17]

_Battlefield 6_'s Battle Pass opens with a five-tier "Recruit" path before the four main paths unlock.[^3] Whatever the intent, that is the structure of endowed progress. So are the fifteen-minute XP boosts EA added specifically into Career Ranks 3 to 22 — the onboarding window, where the gradient is weakest and the player is deciding whether to stay.[^2]

### Progress decoupled from proficiency

One detail deserves attention. Hardware XP accrues _passively_ for merely holding a weapon or sitting in a vehicle — less than for kills, but nonzero.[^2] The number therefore rises without skill. A badge at Hardware Rank 30 certifies time, and the system calls it mastery.

This matters more than it looks: the sign of attainment has been detached from attainment. Chögyam Trungpa's diagnosis of spiritual materialism names the same structure — the accumulation of credentials of attainment in place of the thing itself.[^18] A cabinet of 228 badges is that without the religion.

### A receding horizon

Functional unlocks stop at Career Rank 50. Cosmetics carry to 100. Beyond that ranks continue "into the thousands," and the badge total grows every season.[^2] The ladder does not end; it is not designed to end.

For a goal-gradient system this is load-bearing. A gradient pulls toward a horizon, and a horizon that recedes pulls indefinitely. Completion is not available — which is the structural reason the satisfaction of an unlock is so brief. There was never a terminus to arrive at.

### Conditions authored by an interested party

EA states openly that progression rates are tuned using in-game progression data and community feedback, and lists the adjustments: faster passive Hardware XP, reduced Hardware XP requirements, larger match-completion bonuses, a quickened Battle Pass earn rate, reworked challenges.[^2] Seasonal rewards expire at season's end.[^3]

No conspiracy is needed; it is in the patch notes. But it is worth naming precisely. The _paccaya_ — the conditions under which craving arises, for millions of players — are being empirically optimised by a party with a commercial interest in the outcome. A practitioner examining their own mind here is examining a mind whose environment was fitted to it by measurement.

## Bhava-taṇhā: the craving to become

The first sermon distinguishes three cravings: for sense pleasure (_kāma-taṇhā_), for becoming or existence (_bhava-taṇhā_), and for non-becoming (_vibhava-taṇhā_).[^19] Most commentary on games reaches for the first. The unlock ladder is the second.

Nothing about Career Rank 34 is sensually pleasant. There is no taste, no sight worth seeing, barely an image. What it offers is a change in what one _is_ — a rank, a title, a class of person who has the M4A1 and the EOD Bot. In the twelve links, clinging conditions becoming, becoming conditions birth, and birth conditions [[Suffering|the whole mass of suffering]]. Every unlock is a small birth, and the flatness that follows within about ninety seconds is its ageing and death, on a schedule compressed enough to observe in a single evening.

This is why the two engines need separating. The death-and-respawn loop runs on aversion; the rank ladder runs on becoming. They are different links of one chain, they reinforce each other, and a practice instruction addressing only one will miss the other entirely.

### The account as ātman

A round of _Battlefield 6_ is radically impermanent. It ends, the score resets, nothing carries. The progression system exists to contradict that. Career Rank, Hardware Rank, badges, dog tags, accolades, playercard title and background are the machinery by which a continuous, owned, cumulative self is stitched across otherwise disconnected sessions. The round is _anicca_. The profile is built to feel like it is not.

The game's own vocabulary says so without embarrassment: Career, mastery, bragging rights, pride, "This is Your War."[^2] It is a self-project with a public ledger — a reasonable description of what the tradition means by _sakkāya-diṭṭhi_, the view of a substantial self, here not as a philosophical error but as a designed and pleasurable one.

## Merit, badges, and the Emperor Wu problem

The XP ledger invites comparison with merit, and the tradition's sharpest image for the problem is itself a story about a ledger.

[[Merit]] (_puñña_) accumulates through skilful action and shapes future conditions; lay practice across Theravada countries makes merit-making daily, visible and countable. Mahayana holds merit and wisdom as two accumulations jointly required for awakening, so merit pursued for its own sake settles into accumulation alone.[^20]

The sharpest version of that criticism is the exchange between Bodhidharma and Emperor Wu of Liang: the emperor lists the temples built and the monks ordained, asks what merit he has earned, and is told there is none.

### What kind of story it is

The story stays. What needs stating is the kind of claim it makes, because _did this happen_ is not a question it was built to answer.

Encounter dialogue is a Song-dynasty literary form, and a _gongan_ is a case, not testimony; attribution inside that genre names a paradigm rather than an author, which is why "attributed to" is the accurate formula and not a weaker one.[^23] The earliest sources bear this out. The biographical notice recorded by Bodhidharma's disciple Tanlin, and Daoxuan's _Continued Biographies of Eminent Monks_ (645, revised to 664), contain no imperial audience at all; they have him arrive at Canton and travel north to Luoyang, attracting few followers with a difficult method of "wall contemplation." Bernard Faure reads the meeting with Emperor Wu as a hagiographical topos — structurally a "duel" — and a variant of an existing story about Fu Xi (497–569) and the same emperor, with the thaumaturge Baozhi as clairvoyant witness in both. His conclusion is not that the material should be discarded but that Bodhidharma is to be read as a textual and religious paradigm rather than reconstructed as a person.[^21] John McRae puts the same point as the first of his rules of Zen studies: it is not true, and therefore it is more important.

The exchange reaches canonical form in Song [[Zen|Chan]] literature, and sits in Yuanwu's commentary framing _Blue Cliff Record_ 1 (1125) rather than in the main case, which begins a beat later with the question about the highest meaning of the holy truths.[^22]

That placement is worth keeping in view, because it tells us who made the story and when. Song Chan monasteries ran on lay donation and imperial patronage. The exchange is therefore not an outsider's attack on merit-making but an institution auditing its own reward economy, in the voice of its founder, at the moment it was most dependent on the thing being criticised — a closer precedent for what this note attempts than a literal sixth-century encounter would have been. A player citing Career Rank, badge total and K/D is Emperor Wu with a scoreboard, and the criticism comes from inside the house.

There is a neatness here the story seems to have anticipated. To ask whether the encounter really happened is to ask for an accounting of its merit, and the answer comes back the same as the emperor's.

Two limits, plainly. The story cannot do evidential work about the sixth century, and does not need to: the doctrinal claim it illustrates — that merit without wisdom settles into accumulation — rests on its own sources.[^20] And merit theory concerns intention and its ripening across a moral universe, where XP concerns an integer in a database maintained by a publisher. What transfers is not a metaphysics but a warning: accumulation with a ledger tends to become a self-project, and the ledger is the mechanism by which it does.

## What the frame must not flatten

A Buddhist reading that treats the whole activity as _kilesa_ would be both unkind and empirically wrong.

Self-determination theory research finds that the appeal of games rests on satisfaction of autonomy, competence and relatedness — and, in a direct test, that violent content does not itself drive enjoyment or immersion. Players high in trait aggression preferred violent games but did not enjoy them more for the violence.[^24] [^25] Squad play — reviving, resupplying, spotting, holding a flank for someone else — is a relatedness structure, and _Battlefield 6_ pays Career XP for every one of those cooperative acts as readily as for kills.[^2] A squadmate's good play producing genuine gladness is not far from _muditā_. Competence is not a defilement; mastery is not craving.

Christoph Bareither's ethnography of emotional practice in violent games makes the point from the humanities side: player experience is a complex, learned, socially organised set of practices, not a single appetite.[^26]

Clinical caution is also warranted. Gaming disorder is a real ICD-11 category (6C51), requiring impaired control, escalating priority over other activities, continuation despite harm, and normally twelve months' duration with significant functional impairment.[^27] It is also contested: a body of scholars argued that the diagnosis risks pathologising ordinary behaviour on a thin evidence base.[^28] Most players are not disordered, and the Buddhist analysis of craving does not require them to be — it applies to the ordinary mind at ordinary intensity.

## Absorption is not samādhi

The most seductive error for a practitioner is to identify game absorption with meditative concentration. They share surface features: one-pointedness, time distortion, loss of self-consciousness, effortlessness at the right challenge level.

The evidence runs the other way. Sheldon, Prentice and Halusic found mindfulness and flow absorption negatively associated across trait, diary, experience-sampling and experimental data — including an experiment in which a mindfulness induction _reduced_ flow while participants played a game. The antagonism attached specifically to the absorption component, not to the sense of control.[^29] Flow dissolves the observer; [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]] constitutes one.

The doctrinal contrast is equally clean. _Jhāna_ is described as arising from seclusion from sense pleasures and unwholesome states, resting on ethical foundation, and withdrawing from sensory input.[^13] Game absorption is absorption _into_ sense objects, sustained by threat and uncertainty and dependent on them. Structurally it is the near enemy of concentration: close enough in feel to be mistaken for it, opposite in direction.

## Virtual killing and cetanā

On the precept question the canonical criterion is intention: _cetanāhaṃ, bhikkhave, kammaṃ vadāmi_ — intention, the Buddha says, is kamma; having intended, one acts by body, speech and mind.[^30] The commentarial analysis of killing requires a living being and its actual death among the constituting factors, neither of which obtains against a respawning model.

So the useful question is not whether a precept was broken but what disposition is being trained. [[Karma|Kamma]] includes mental action, and rehearsal shapes _saṅkhārā_. Two players performing identical inputs may be cultivating quite different states: tactical problem-solving and cooperative attention in one case, rehearsed contempt and the pleasure of domination in the other. The Mathiak item analysis is suggestive — the adjectives predicting temporal-pole reactivity clustered around anger, hostility and irritability, and it was the players with _weaker_ regulatory response who left the session in worse mood.[^7]

The progression layer adds a second ethical question with nothing to do with violence: what is trained by hours of action performed because a bar moves. Grinding a weapon one does not enjoy, to reach an attachment one does not need, to fill a badge slot before a season expires, is a rehearsal of instrumental relation to one's own time.

The unambiguous ethical surface, meanwhile, is not the shooting. It is voice and text chat and conduct toward the other human beings in the server, where the beings are real, the speech is real, and the precepts apply straightforwardly.

Religious studies has begun to treat games as a site of genuine practice and meaning rather than a distraction from it, which is the right register for the whole question.[^31]

## Questions to carry into a session

- What is the _vedanā_ at the death screen, before the decision to queue again?
- Is the next round chosen, or is it the removal of an unpleasant feeling-tone?
- Where is the gratification (_assāda_) honestly located — in mastery, in company, in relief, in the number?
- When the unlock lands, how long does the satisfaction last? Time it once.
- Would this session have happened without the bar? If not, whose intention is being enacted?
- What does the ledger say I have become, and who is the one it says that about?
- What is the state of mind twenty minutes after stopping, and what does that say about the baseline?

## Limits of the evidence

No neuroimaging or psychological study has used _Battlefield V_ or _Battlefield 6_. The closest work used a different shooter, thirteen male participants and 2011 methods. Claims about _Battlefield 6_'s progression structure rest on the publisher's own documentation, which describes the design but not its effects. The ramping-dopamine result comes from rodents navigating mazes; its extension to a progress bar is an argument, not a finding. The violent-games literature is contested, with meta-analytic effects disputed on publication-bias grounds. The Buddhist material is applied analogically: nothing in the Pali Canon concerns simulated combat, and the fit between _vedanā_-based dependent origination and reward prediction error, while striking, is a structural parallel argued by contemporary clinicians, not a demonstrated identity.
