# The Mind as Mirror

If the Buddha is formless yet every form — see [[Trikaya#Formless as All Forms|Formless as All Forms]] — where does the mind stand? The tradition's recurring answer is the mirror: a mind that reflects every form while being none of them. It is _formless as all forms_, seen from the inside.

## Mirror Wisdom

In Yogācāra, a Buddha land is not a place. It is a symbol of the mind of wisdom, made of five factors: the Pure Dharma Realm and four wisdoms.[^1]

| Wisdom | Japanese | Sanskrit | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mirror wisdom | 大円鏡智 _daienkyōchi_ | _ādarśa-jñāna_ | Reflects all things without distortion or selection |
| Equality wisdom | 平等性智 _byōdōshōchi_ | _samatā-jñāna_ | Sees self and others as equal |
| Discernment wisdom | 妙観察智 _myōkanzatchi_ | _pratyavekṣaṇā-jñāna_ | Distinguishes each thing clearly |
| Duty-fulfilment wisdom | 成所作智 _jōshosachi_ | _kṛtyānuṣṭhāna-jñāna_ | Acts in the world for beings |

The first two wisdoms are non-discriminating. The last two discern the bodhisattva's tasks and carry them out.[^1] Mirror wisdom is the purest image of that idea: a surface that receives every form, holds none, and is not changed by any.

## Kūkai's Five Wisdoms

Shingon adds a fifth, 法界体性智 (_hokkai taishōchi_, wisdom of the essential nature of the Dharma realm), which belongs to Dainichi himself. Each wisdom is then embodied by one of the five Buddhas of the Diamond Realm mandala.[^2]

| Buddha | Wisdom |
| --- | --- |
| Dainichi (大日) | 法界体性智 — the Dharma realm itself |
| Ashuku (阿閦, Akṣobhya) | 大円鏡智 — mirror wisdom |
| Hōshō (宝生, Ratnasambhava) | 平等性智 — equality wisdom |
| Amida (阿弥陀, Amitābha) | 妙観察智 — discernment wisdom |
| Fukūjōju (不空成就, Amoghasiddhi) | 成所作智 — duty-fulfilment wisdom |

In this scheme the mirror is not a metaphor added to the Buddha. It is one of the Buddha's own faces.

## To Know One's Own Mind as It Is

The _Mahāvairocana Sutra_ defines awakening as 如実知自心: "to know one's own mind as it truly is."[^3] Kūkai builds on this. Mind (識, consciousness) is the sixth of the six great elements, so the mirror is made of the same Buddha-body as the forms it reflects.[^4] Mirror and reflection are not two.

This joins the mirror to the _Shōji jissō gi_ (see [[Kukai, the Letter A, and the Dragon Girl]]). The mind that reads the world's letters and knows their source is the mind that knows itself.

## The Moon Disc

In _Ajikan_ (阿字観), the practitioner visualises a full moon disc, the pure, bright nature of the mind, with the letter _A_ upon it. The disc expands to fill the universe and then returns.[^5] Like a mirror, the moon lights everything and holds nothing.

## A Zen Counterpoint

The _Platform Sutra_ (六祖壇経) warns against making the mirror into a thing. Shenxiu's verse says 心如明鏡台，時時勤拂拭: the mind is like a bright mirror-stand, to be wiped constantly. Huineng answers 明鏡亦非台，本來無一物: the bright mirror has no stand, and originally there is not a single thing.[^6]

This echoes the _hongaku_ (original enlightenment) debate described in [[Trikaya]]. One side says to polish the mirror, the path of practice. The other says there is no mirror to polish, original awakening.

## Reflection, Not Projection

One way to read these images together, offered here as interpretation rather than doctrine, is this. Yogācāra also teaches "mind-only" (唯識, _yuishiki_): the deluded mind _projects_ its world. Mirror wisdom is what that same mind becomes when awakened. It no longer projects, and simply _reflects_ things as they are. On this reading, awakening is the shift from projection to reflection.
