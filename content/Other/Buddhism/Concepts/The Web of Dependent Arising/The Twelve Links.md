# The Twelve Links

## About this thread

This note opens a research thread on the twelve links (_nidāna_) of dependent origination, 十二因縁 (_jūni innen_) in Japanese.[^15] It gathers what the earlier research already established and sets out the questions still open. The general principle and its link to emptiness are covered in [[Dependent Origination and Emptiness]]; this note stays with the chain itself.[^1]

## The links at a glance

SN 12.2 (_Vibhaṅga Sutta_) is the discourse that defines each link. It gives the definitions in reverse order, from old age and death back to ignorance. The table puts them in the usual forward order. Chinese names are from the parallel _Saṃyuktāgama_ 298.[^2][^3]

| # | Pāli | Chinese (SĀ 298) | Sujato | SN 12.2 definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | _avijjā_ | 無明 | ignorance | "Not knowing about suffering, the origin of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering." (14.2) |
| 2 | _saṅkhārā_ | 行 | choices | "Choices by way of body, speech, and mind." (13.3) |
| 3 | _viññāṇa_ | 識 | consciousness | "Eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind consciousness." (12.3) |
| 4 | _nāmarūpa_ | 名色 | name and form | Name: "Feeling, perception, intention, contact, and application of mind." (11.2). Form: "The four principal states, and form derived from the four principal states." (11.4) |
| 5 | _saḷāyatana_ | 六入處 | six sense fields | "The sense fields of the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind." (10.2) |
| 6 | _phassa_ | 觸 | contact | "Contact through the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind." (9.3) |
| 7 | _vedanā_ | 受 | feeling | "Feeling born of contact through the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind." (8.3) |
| 8 | _taṇhā_ | 愛 | craving | "Craving for sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas." (7.3) |
| 9 | _upādāna_ | 取 | grasping | "Grasping at sensual pleasures, views, precepts and observances, and theories of a self." (6.3) |
| 10 | _bhava_ | 有 | continued existence | "Existence in the sensual realm, the realm of luminous form, and the formless realm." (5.3) |
| 11 | _jāti_ | 生 | rebirth | "The rebirth, inception, conception, regeneration, manifestation of the aggregates, and acquisition of the sense fields of the various sentient beings in the various orders of sentient beings." (4.2) |
| 12 | _jarāmaraṇa_ | 老死 | old age and death | Old age: "The old age, decrepitude, broken teeth, grey hair, wrinkly skin, diminished vitality, and failing faculties of the various sentient beings in the various orders of sentient beings." (3.2). Death: "The passing away, passing on, disintegration, demise, mortality, death, decease, breaking up of the aggregates, and laying to rest of the corpse of the various sentient beings in the various orders of sentient beings." (3.4) |

Sujato's "choices" for _saṅkhārā_ and "requirement" for _paccaya_ are his own renderings. Others give "volitional formations" and "condition".

### How SN 12.2 is built

SN 12.2 moves in three steps. It states the chain forward (2.2–2.12), defines each link from the last back to the first (sections 3–14), then sums up both directions: arising (15.1–15.3) and ceasing (15.4–15.6).[^2]

| Step | Sections | Direction | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2.2–2.12 | Forward | The chain is stated: ignorance → … → old age and death |
| 2 | 3–14 | Backward | Each link is defined, from old age and death back to ignorance |
| 3 | 15.1–15.6 | Both | Arising is summed up (15.1–15.3), then ceasing (15.4–15.6) |

### Open chain or closed wheel

![[Other/Buddhism/Concepts/SN 12.2 Open Chain and Wheel Diagram v1.svg]]

_The twelve links as SN 12.2 orders them, beside the wheel of the commentary and the painting, in Claude's framing, drawn from SuttaCentral's edition. Gold: where the commentary joins the end of the chain to its start._

In SN 12.2 the chain has a first term and a last: it runs from ignorance to old age and death, and nothing in the sutta leads from old age and death back to ignorance (2.2–2.12, 15.1–15.6).[^2] The closed circle comes from elsewhere.

**Ignorance has its own condition.** The suttas do not treat ignorance as uncaused. In MN 9 Sāriputta names defilement (_āsava_) as the origin of ignorance, and ignorance as the origin of defilement. The two condition each other, outside the twelve links (66.1, 70.4):[^17]

> Ignorance originates from defilement. Ignorance ceases when defilement ceases. The practice that leads to the cessation of ignorance is simply this noble eightfold path …”
>
> \[...]
>
> Defilement originates from ignorance. Defilement ceases when ignorance ceases. The practice that leads to the cessation of defilement is simply this noble eightfold path, that is:
>
> _Āsavasamudayā avijjāsamudayo, āsavanirodhā avijjānirodho, ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo avijjānirodhagāminī paṭipadā, seyyathidaṁ—_
>
> \[...]
>
> _Avijjāsamudayā āsavasamudayo, avijjānirodhā āsavanirodho, ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā, seyyathidaṁ—_

**The commentary closes the circle.** Buddhaghosa calls the twelve links a "wheel of becoming" (_bhavacakka_) whose beginning is not known, and joins its end to its start. Sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness and distress, which SN 12.2 lists with old age and death (2.12), arise from the defilements. The defilements are the cause of ignorance, so wherever sorrow and the rest are found, ignorance is found too, and the wheel turns again. For the step from defilement to ignorance he cites MN 9.[^18]

**The painting makes it a picture.** On the painted _bhavacakra_ the links run all the way round the rim, so old age and death sit next to ignorance (see [[Wheel of Life]]). Brasington describes the image link by link (pp. 24–37). Teiser traces its written and painted forms from India to China.[^19]

The open chain shows the sutta's own shape. The closed wheel is the reading of the commentary and the painting, which give ignorance a cause of its own.

## Pāli and Chinese definitions compared

SĀ 298 is not a word-for-word match for SN 12.2. Four definitions differ:

- **Name (_nāma_).** SN 12.2 lists feeling, perception, intention, contact and attention (11.2). SĀ 298 gives the four immaterial aggregates: 緣識名色者，云何名？謂四無色陰——受陰、想陰、行陰、識陰。 ("As for 'consciousness conditions name-and-form', what is name? The four immaterial aggregates: feeling, perception, formations and consciousness.")
- **Feeling (_vedanā_).** SN 12.2 classifies feeling by the six senses (8.3). SĀ 298 classifies it by tone: 緣觸受者，云何為受？謂三受——苦受、樂受、不苦不樂受。 ("As for 'contact conditions feeling', what is feeling? The three feelings: painful, pleasant, and neither painful nor pleasant.")
- **Craving (_taṇhā_).** SN 12.2 classifies craving by its six objects (7.3). SĀ 298 classifies it by realm: 緣受愛者，彼云何為愛？謂三愛——欲愛、色愛、無色愛。 ("As for 'feeling conditions craving', what is craving? The three cravings: for the sensual, for form, and for the formless.")
- **Ignorance (_avijjā_).** SN 12.2 defines it as not knowing the four noble truths (14.2). SĀ 298 gives a much longer list: not knowing past and future, inside and outside, action and result, the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha, the four truths, causes and what arises from them, and more (85a16–25).

The English renderings of the Chinese are Claude's. The Tibetan version below, in 84000's translation, gives the same four definitions as SĀ 298.

## The Tibetan versions (84000)

84000 has translated the two Kangyur sūtras on the twelve links: Toh 211, the Tibetan counterpart of the analysis in SN 12.2 and SĀ 298, and Toh 210, the Mahāyāna _Rice Seedling_.[^13][^14] The passages below are taken from 84000's published TEI files, the source of its online reading room, and checked character for character against them.

### Toh 211: the analysis of the links

84000's introduction places the Sanskrit of this sūtra in the Sarvāstivāda _Nidānasaṃyukta_ and notes that the Tibetan has no colophon, though it appears in the Denkarma inventory of about 812 CE. The introduction also says this sūtra is not among the _Nidānasaṃyukta_ texts shared with the Pāli collection.[^13] ==Śraddhāpa Shan Welsh and Jones, by contrast, compare eight versions of the analysis in Pāli, Sanskrit, Tibetan and Chinese (abstract only; article not yet read)==.[^16]

On all four points where SĀ 298 differs from SN 12.2, Toh 211 sides with SĀ 298. Its ignorance is the long list (past and future, inner and outer, actions and their maturation, the Three Jewels, the four truths, causes, and more). Name, feeling and craving:

> “What is name in the statement, ‘Through the condition of consciousness, name-and-form arises’? It is the four formless aggregates. They are the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, and the aggregate of consciousness. ([passage](https://84000.co/translation/toh211#UT22084-062-011-22))
>
> “What is feeling in the statement, ‘Through the condition of contact, feeling arises’? There are three feelings: pleasure, pain, and neither pleasure nor pain. ([passage](https://84000.co/translation/toh211#UT22084-062-011-27))
>
> “What is craving in the statement, ‘Through the condition of feeling, craving arises’? There are three cravings: the craving of the desire realm, the craving of the form realm, and the craving of the formless realm. ([passage](https://84000.co/translation/toh211#UT22084-062-011-28))

84000 renders the links as ignorance, formations, consciousness, name-and-form, six sense sources, contact, feeling, craving, grasping, existence, birth, and aging and death.

### Toh 210: the _Rice Seedling_

Maitreya explains to Śāriputra the Buddha's saying that whoever sees dependent arising sees the Dharma. Here the translators use "sensation" for _vedanā_, "appropriation" for _upādāna_ and "becoming" for _bhava_. The sūtra glosses each link by what it does:

> “They are called ignorance in the sense of obscuring, formations in the sense of forming, consciousness in the sense of knowing, name and form in the sense of mutual support, the six sense sources in the sense of entryways, contact in the sense of contact, sensation in the sense of experience, craving in the sense of thirst, appropriation in the sense of appropriating, becoming in the sense of giving birth to repeated becoming, birth in the sense of the emergence of the aggregates, aging in the sense of the maturation of the aggregates, death in the sense of perishing, sorrow in the sense of grieving, lamentation in the sense of wailing, suffering in the sense of bodily torment, despair in the sense of mental torment, and anxiety in the sense of subtle defilement. ([passage](https://84000.co/translation/toh210#UT22084-062-010-62))

It then names four links that hold the chain together, and gives them the image of a seed in a field:

> “Consciousness functions as a cause by having the nature of a seed. Karma functions as a cause by having the nature of a field. Ignorance and craving function as causes by having the nature of afflictions. ([passage](https://84000.co/translation/toh210#UT22084-062-010-85))
>
> “Karma and afflictions cause the seed of consciousness to grow. Here, karma functions as the field for the seed of consciousness. Craving moistens the seed of consciousness. Ignorance sows the seed of consciousness. Without these conditions, the seed of consciousness does not develop. ([passage](https://84000.co/translation/toh210#UT22084-062-010-86))

## The chain in two directions

SN 12.2 closes with both directions, arising and ceasing (15.1–15.6). The general formula behind them (when this exists, that comes to be; when this ceases, that ceases) is in SN 12.21 and quoted in full in [[Dependent Origination and Emptiness]].[^4]

### Beyond suffering: the "vital conditions" (SN 12.23)

The _Upanisa Sutta_ runs the twelve links and then keeps going. Suffering becomes the condition for faith, and a second chain leads to liberation. The turning point, segments 6.11–6.12:

> Rebirth is the vital condition for suffering. Suffering is the vital condition for faith.
>
> _jātūpanisaṁ dukkhaṁ, dukkhūpanisā saddhā,_[^5]

From faith the chain runs through joy, rapture, tranquillity, bliss, immersion, truly knowing and seeing, disillusionment, dispassion and freedom to the knowledge that the defilements have ended (2.1–3.15, read in reverse). Brasington calls this transcendental dependent origination and argues that it cannot be spread over several lifetimes (pp. 42–43).[^6] For a fuller treatment he points to Bhikkhu Bodhi's exposition of the sutta (p. 43, n. 1; see Still to read).

## How the links are read

### Across lives or in each moment

- **Three lives.** The Theravāda commentary, set out in Buddhaghosa's _Visuddhimagga_ (ch. 17), puts ignorance and choices in the past life, links 3–10 in this life, and birth and old age and death in the next. Jones calls it anachronistic to read this back into the early discourses, and notes that Theravāda and Sarvāstivāda each had their own version.[^7]
- **Moment to moment.** All twelve links run with each sense contact. Brasington takes this from Buddhadāsa (pp. 40–41).
- **Any scale.** Varela, Thompson and Rosch: "The circle is an analytic structure that can be used to describe events of any duration from a single moment to a lifetime or, in the Buddhist view, to many lifetimes." (p. 111)[^8]

**A point for the three-life side.** SN 12.2's own definitions of birth and of old age and death are biological: birth as conception and the appearance of the aggregates (4.2), death as the break-up of the aggregates and the laying down of the body (3.4). A moment-to-moment reading has to take these links figuratively, as the birth and death of a sense of self. Brasington does exactly this with his "mango bringer" example (p. 40).

### Where the chain can be broken

| Break point | Source | Reason given |
|---|---|---|
| Feeling → craving | Brasington (pp. 19, 40) | Contact and feeling arise automatically; craving need not follow |
| Craving → grasping | Varela, Thompson and Rosch (p. 114) | "Up to this point, the links have rolled off automatically on the basis of past conditioning." |
| Ignorance, at the root | SN 12.2 (15.4); Brasington (p. 40) | With ignorance gone, the whole chain ceases |

### One chain or a web

- **Linear.** Each link conditions the next.
- **Mutual.** Consciousness and name-and-form lean on each other like two sheaves of reeds (SN 12.67).[^9] Varela, Thompson and Rosch read every link as conditioning and conditioned by the others (p. 110).
- **Caution.** Anālayo warns against sliding from specific conditionality to the idea that everything depends on everything, which he traces to later Huayan thought.[^10]

## History of the formula

- **Variant lists.** Canonical lists of the links differ in length and starting point. Bucknell argues the twelve-link form developed from shorter lists.[^11] DN 15, for example, has fewer links and leaves out the six sense fields (Brasington, p. 39).
- **An earlier sequence.** Brasington treats the chain in Snp 4.11 (quarrels ← what is dear ← desire ← pleasant and unpleasant ← contact ← name-and-form) as the earliest teaching on dependent origination (pp. 16–19).
- **Vedic background.** Jurewicz reads the twelve links against Vedic accounts of creation.[^12] She argues the chain was framed as a polemic against Vedic cosmogony; see [[Dependent Origination and Vedic Thought]].
- **The Wheel of Life.** The _bhavacakra_ image places the twelve links on its outer rim. Brasington describes it link by link (pp. 24–37). Varela, Thompson and Rosch's figure 6.1 (p. 112) maps the links onto past, present and future, and onto action (_karma_) and affliction (_kleśa_).

## Open questions for this thread

1. **Definitions across traditions.** Compare SN 12.2 and SĀ 298 with the Sanskrit _Nidānasaṃyukta_ (Chung and Fukita's 2020 edition is cited by Jones) and with the _Pratītyasamutpādavibhaṅga Sūtra_. The Tibetan Toh 211 is now above; the Sanskrit and the Shan Welsh and Jones article remain.
2. **The Abhidharma readings.** Set Buddhaghosa's three-life scheme (_Visuddhimagga_ XVII) beside Vasubandhu's _Abhidharmakośabhāṣya_ III, which reads the links in more than one way.
3. **Each link in detail.** Go link by link through the sutta definitions, the commentaries and modern readings. The obvious places to start are _saṅkhārā_ (choices, formations, fabrications), _nāmarūpa_ and _bhava_.
4. **Where to intervene.** Test the three break points against the suttas on mindfulness of feeling and on craving, and against [[Mental and Conceptual Proliferation - prapañca - papanca - Chaotic Thoughts]].
5. **Science, link by link.** Look for careful parallels, not proofs: contact and feeling alongside affective valence; craving and grasping alongside research on wanting and habit.
6. **Japan.** How 十二因縁 is taught in the Japanese schools, and how the Wheel of Life is depicted.
7. **Diagram.** A twelve-links diagram in the vault's house style, showing both directions and the break points.
