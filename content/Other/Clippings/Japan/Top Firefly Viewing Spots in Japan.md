## ==8 Tsukiyono Firefly Village (Gunma)==

==Located just a three-minute walk from Jomo-Kogen Station in Minakami Town, Tsukiyono Firefly Village is one of the most beloved viewing spots in the Kanto region. Known for its clean mountain air and pristine water sources, the area supports thriving populations of at least three firefly species, including the iconic \*Genji-botaru\* (Genji firefly).==

==Thanks to the efforts of a local conservation group, this once-dwindling population is now flourishing. Each summer, from mid-June to mid-July, thousands of fireflies dance along the riverside paths and over gently flowing streams. Visitors are invited to walk a 1.9-kilometer promenade through the countryside, softly illuminated to preserve the insects’ natural rhythm.==

==Volunteer guides from the firefly preservation society are on hand during viewing hours (typically 8–9 p.m.) to point out the best viewing areas and share insights into the fireflies’ life cycle. Surrounded by forested hills and farmland of \[Gunma,]\(https://travel.gaijinpot.com/destination/gunma/) this Firefly viewing spot offers a peaceful, immersive experience just a short journey from \[Tokyo.]\(https://travel.gaijinpot.com/destination/tokyo/)==

==\*\*Firefly Types:\*\* Genji, Heike, Kuromao==

==\*\*Closest Station:\*\* Jomo-Kogen==

## ==7 Hotel Chinzanso Tokyo (Tokyo)==

==A rare oasis in the heart of \[Tokyo]\(https://travel.gaijinpot.com/destination/tokyo/), Hotel Chinzanso is known for its lush, historic Japanese garden that is home to a long-running firefly conservation project. Since 1954, the hotel has been carefully raising fireflies in \[Nagano Prefecture]\(https://travel.gaijinpot.com/destination/nagano/), then releasing them into its beautifully maintained garden each winter. By late May, thousands of these glowing insects begin to emerge, particularly around the iconic Benkei Bridge and the garden’s streams, creating a magical summer spectacle in the city.==

==Firefly season at Chinzanso typically runs from mid-May to the end of June, with peak viewing after sunset. The experience is not limited to hotel guests, as visitors with dinner reservations at one of the garden-view restaurants can also enjoy the show. Special seasonal menus and “firefly-themed” dining experiences are also offered during this time. Booking early is recommended, as this seasonal event is a popular choice for couples.==

\============\*\*Firefly Type:\*\* Genji============

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Edogawabashi==

## ==6 Fussa Firefly Park (Tokyo)==

==Just a short train ride from central Tokyo, Fussa Firefly Park offers a nostalgic escape into early summer magic. Home to one of Tokyo’s longest-running hotaru festivals, this annual event has been lighting up summer nights for over 60 years. Each year, hundreds of Genji fireflies are carefully released into the park by local volunteers and caretakers dedicated to preserving this tradition.==

\==This event is celebrated by the community with a festival that features food stalls offering classic summer treats, local _taiko_ (drumming) performances and other family-friendly festival activities. The relaxed atmosphere draws both locals and travellers looking to experience a traditional Japanese summer festival.\
For the best view of the fireflies, plan to arrive by 8 p.m., when they’re most active. The event typically winds down by 10 p.m., so early arrival is recommended, especially if you want to explore the festival and secure a good viewing spot.==

**Firefly Type:** Genji

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Ushihama==

## ==5 Kemi Firefly Village (Nagano)==

==Set deep in the nature-rich hills of Nagano Prefecture, Kemi Firefly Village is a hidden gem lovingly maintained by the local community. Each year, residents raise and release firefly larvae into the clear streams that run through the area, helping to sustain the delicate ecosystem that supports their glow.==

==The village comes alive during the annual Firefly Festival, when thousands of fireflies light up the evening. Visitors can stroll along quiet rural paths and listen to the sounds of flowing water and gentle breezes as the fireflies begin their nightly dance. The untouched atmosphere, combined with the dedication of the villagers, creates a warm and welcoming setting that feels worlds away from city life.==

**Firefly Type:** Genji

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Shinano Matsukawa==

## ==4 Tatsuno Hotaru Doyo Park (Nagano)==

==Widely regarded as one of Japan’s top firefly destinations, Tatsuno Town in Nagano comes alive each June with the gentle glow of thousands of fireflies. For more than 70 years, the community has hosted the Tatsuno Firefly Festival. This vibrant celebration reflects Tatsuno’s deep-rooted connection to its land and traditions. Locals celebrate the careful conservation of the area’s wetlands and water sources that allow the Genji fireflies to thrive.==

==Nestled in the Ina Valley, Hotaru Doyo Park offers an enchanting setting where fireflies light up riverside grasses and forest paths. The viewing season typically peaks in mid-June and the festival draws visitors from across the country. Whether you’re drawn by the glowing insects or the lively festival spirit, a visit to Tatsuno offers a rare blend of nature, heritage and heartfelt community celebration.==

**Firefly Type:** Genji

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Tatsuno==

## ==3 Hokubo Firefly Park (Okayama)==

\==Nestled along the Bitchu River in Maniwa City, Hokubo Firefly Park is one of Japan’s largest and most naturally preserved firefly habitats. Stretching across several kilometers of riverbank, this area has earned recognition from Japan’s Ministry of the Environment as a designated “Village of Living Things” for its thriving biodiversity and careful conservation efforts.\
From early to late June, thousands of Genji fireflies light up the river. The sight is especially breathtaking on warm, still evenings, when the air is quiet and the fireflies’ glow reflects off the water’s surface.==

==Families and nature lovers will appreciate the small but informative displays set up around the site, which offer insight into the lifecycle and behavior of these delicate insects. The peaceful rural setting and commitment to ecological education make Hokubo in Okayama Prefecture a rewarding destination for those seeking a more immersive firefly-viewing experience.==

**Firefly Type:** Genji

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Mimasaka-Ochiai (There is no nearby station; this location is best visited by car/taxi)==

## ==2 Tenno Hachiman Shrine (Okayama)==

==Hidden in the forests of Niimi City, Tenno Hachiman Shrine offers a truly unique firefly viewing experience. This centuries-old shrine is dedicated to Hachiman, the Shinto god of war and protector of communities and has long served as a spiritual center for the region. The surrounding forest has remained largely untouched, helping preserve the delicate ecosystem that supports a rare species of firefly, himebotaru, also known as kinbotaru (golden fireflies).==

==These tiny fireflies emit a rapid golden flash, creating a dazzling, starlike effect deep within the woods. Their appearance is fleeting, typically only for 10 days around July 10, making sightings all the more special.==

==Designated a natural monument of \[Okayama Prefecture]\(https://travel.gaijinpot.com/destination/okayama/), the shrine grounds are carefully protected. Visitors are asked to move quietly, avoid artificial lights and respect the serene atmosphere. The combination of sacred space, ancient forest and the magical glow of golden fireflies creates one of the most ethereal firefly-viewing experiences in Japan.==

==\*\*Firefly Type:\*\* Kinbotaru (Golden Firefly)==

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Niimi (JR Hakubi Line)==

## ==1 Aioiyama Ryokuchi (Nagoya)==

==Tucked into a quiet residential area of Nagoya City, Aioiyama Ryokuchi is a hidden gem for late-night firefly viewing. The park is surrounded by bamboo groves, orchards and wooded trails that create the perfect low-light environment for fireflies. What sets this spot apart is its very late peak. Fireflies often emerge between 9 p.m. and 11:30 p.m., offering a peaceful, crowd-free viewing experience.==

==This spot is lesser known among tourists, making it ideal for those who want a quiet, contemplative evening surrounded by nature in the heart of the city.==

**Firefly Type:** Genji

==\*\*Closest station:\*\* Aioiyama==

## 🔥 Firefly Season in Miyazaki

In Japan, firefly breeding begins in late May, peaks in June, and by late July they are rarely seen — making them a beloved symbol of early Japanese summer. Miyazaki's warm southern climate means the season starts **slightly earlier** than most of Japan.

**Best viewing window in Miyazaki: late May – late June** Peak: **early to mid-June** Best time of night: **8:00 PM – 10:00 PM**

---

## 📍 Best Spots in & Near Miyazaki City

**1. 🌊 Oyodo River Banks — Miyazaki City** The clean riverbanks along the Oyodo River provide natural firefly habitat. Look for quiet, unlit stretches away from the city center after dark.

**2. 🌿 Heiwadai Park Forest Paths — Miyazaki City** The wooded areas and small streams inside Heiwadai Park are known local hotspots for firefly sightings in early June.

**3. 🏞️ Takachiho Gorge Area — Miyazaki Prefecture** The Miyazaki City area has dedicated private garden firefly viewing spots, particularly suited for early-season hotaru viewers. Takachiho's pristine mountain streams are among the best in Kyushu for wild fireflies — a magical combination of gorge scenery and glowing hotaru.

**4. 🌾 Rural Rice Paddy Areas — Sadowara & Kiyotake** The rice paddies and irrigation channels around Sadowara and Kiyotake towns, just north and south of Miyazaki City, attract Heike fireflies from mid-June onward.

---

## 🗓️ Nearby Reference — Fukuoka/Kyushu Dates

Firefly viewing in the broader Kyushu region is running now through mid-June 2026, with fireflies most active between 20:00 and 21:00. Community-led conservation efforts across Kyushu have maintained clean river habitats for Genji, Heike, and Hime firefly species.

---

## 💡 Tips for Miyazaki Firefly Viewing

- Go on **calm, humid evenings** — fireflies dislike wind and rain
- Wear **dark clothing** and avoid phone screens
- The **new moon period** offers the darkest skies — next one is early June
- Firefly larvae can only survive in clean, unpolluted water — spotting them is a sign of a healthy environment. 🌿
