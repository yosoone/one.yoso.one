# Alice in Glitchland

“Wrong mushroom, Echo! Wrong damn dimension! Look for the glitch, the shimmer. It’s… it’s like the world’s buffering, you get me?”
You blink, and the neon glare of Electric Tea spills across your retinas. Not the soft, dappled sunlight of a forgotten garden, but the harsh, pulsating rhythm of Tokyo at midnight. You’re leaning against a graffitied wall in Shibuya, the bass of a distant rave thrumming in your bones. Your ripped fishnets snag on a stray piece of discarded manga, and the taste of synthetic bubblegum lingers on your tongue.

“Echo? Echo?” a voice calls, tinny and distorted through a cracked smartphone speaker. You recognize the frantic edge. That’s Rabbit, always late, always wired. Tonight, he’s practically vibrating with urgency.

“Yeah, Rabbit. I’m here. By the… the mushroom cloud mural. You see it?” You scan the swirling vortex of colour and chaotic figures. No white rabbit in sight, just a sea of glowing screens and hurried footsteps.

“Wrong mushroom, Echo! Wrong damn dimension! Look for the glitch, the shimmer. It’s… it’s like the world’s buffering, you get me?”

Buffering. You know that feeling. Like when your neural implant hiccups, and reality flickers for a disorienting second. You push off the wall, your chrome-tipped boots clicking on the slick pavement. This isn’t Wonderland, not the storybook version anyway. This is Glitchland, a fractured reality where the rules are constantly rewriting themselves, where the digital bleeds into the organic.

You remember the tumble through a cascading wave of corrupted data, a rogue algorithm that spat you out into this hyper-saturated nightmare. You were just chasing a particularly elusive piece of street art, a digital ghost only visible through your augmented reality lenses when the world fractured.

“I see something… a ripple in the air, like heat haze, but… pixelated?” You squint, pushing through the throng of cyberpunks and lost souls.

“That’s it! That’s the tear! The Eye got something… something big. Something that could unravel everything. You gotta get through, Echo. You’re the only one who can.” Rabbit’s voice cracks with static.

The Eye. You haven’t seen it yet, but you’ve felt its influence. The oppressive surveillance drones buzzing overhead, the invasive personalised ads that flicker in your vision, the constant feeling of being watched, analysed, and categorised. This isn’t a whimsical tyrant with playing card soldiers; this is a data-mining overlord, a digital deity who controls the flow of information, the very fabric of this broken reality.

You reach the shimmering anomaly. It feels cold, and electric, like stepping into a live wire. Doubt flickers – are you brave enough for this? Are you even the right person? But Rabbit’s desperate plea echoes in your ears. You’re not some innocent Alice, tumbling into a fantasy. You’re Echo, a glitch in the system, a ghost in the machine. And you’re about to crash the party.

With a surge of defiant energy, you step through the pixelated tears, leaving the reality behind. The world on the other side shimmers, unstable, waiting to be rewritten. Your hands instinctively write the volatile cocktail of code designed to disrupt, to dismantle.

Let the buffering end. It’s time to introduce the Eye to a system error that won’t be able to be patched.
