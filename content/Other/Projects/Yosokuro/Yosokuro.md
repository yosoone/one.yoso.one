## Statement

> I want to look into people's souls with a Leica.
> — Yoso

Natural depth. Colour expresses emotion but can be a distraction / noise — human nature in black and white, nature in colour.

> Reveal the unseen — the spirit in nature, urban landscapes and public spaces; the energy, what you feel, and what will be revealed once you look at the shot. Will you have found a spirit?
> — Y

> Record history through your own eyes.
> — Y

## Concept & context

Document my life — in Japan and while travelling. As a tattoo artist who has had the chance to meet wonderful spirits hidden from the mainstream; as a laymonk and Reiki master living in Japan. The mystical gradually reveals itself as one listens and observes the myriad of forms and souls.

- Not as peaceful and appeasing as [[_Shinsei]].
- Maybe keep shooting and see, one day?
- Can be great for people who miss Japan — discovering it from the other side.
- Purify. Elemental. Reach the essence of the living spirit — hence photography.

### Why black and white?

Although I love colour, I find it a distraction — the five senses can make us blind.

Freezing time to reveal what we have, or have not, witnessed in the moment. In some sense it 'expands' reality.

It has to feel emotionally and physically right. No stalking? (Sometimes?)

Seeing — Illusion — Reality — Meditation. (Colour needed in some?)

## Observations

> Black and white photography in Japan and during my travels in Europe. Anonymity, intimacy, underground, black box.
>
> When I look at it, many images bring me a sense of nostalgia, while others really give me an atmosphere unique to Japan — especially the train journeys, people so close to each other, voyeurism, intimacy in public space. When I catch the camera, sometimes it feels like the beast awakes.
>
> However, some shots bring the light and the mystique, which is what I am seeking: to reveal the unseen and ungraspable in the moment. A split of a second, which reveals presence in stillness. Like chasing ghosts and spirits.
> — Y

> But the great photographs, in my experience, are those magical moments that occur all the time. You barely see them out of the corner of your eye. They just go by you. A moment here. A breath. A quick connection. The magic moment is a gift — just as life is a gift, and your response to it is a gift. It's a friend. It knows you. It comes to you and you recognize it.
> — Harold Feinstein

> Definitely some of the greatest! And for some reason, the timing belongs to you and only you. Will you be conscious enough to press the trigger?
> — Y
