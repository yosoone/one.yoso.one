Here are some examples of paintings that feature nymphs in an erotic context:

1. **Joshua Reynolds - _A Nymph and Cupid_** (late 18th century): This painting depicts a nymph with Cupid, emphasizing themes of sensuality and erotic imagination. The nymph's pose and gaze create an oscillation between modesty and immodesty, making it a significant work in exploring erotic themes during the late 18th century[1](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/reynolds-a-nymph-and-cupid-the-snake-in-the-grass-n00885/essay).

2. **Arnold Böcklin - _Nymphe und Satyr_** (1871): This painting illustrates a mythological scene featuring a nymph and a satyr, which is a common erotic motif in classical mythology. The interaction between these figures often symbolizes unbridled desire and natural passion[7](https://picryl.com/topics/19th+century+paintings+of+nymphs/erotic+art).

3. **John William Waterhouse - _Hylas and the Nymphs_** (1896): This painting depicts Hylas being seduced by water nymphs. While not explicitly erotic, it captures the allure and temptation associated with nymphs in mythology, often interpreted through a lens of desire and sensuality[4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylas_and_the_Nymphs_\(painting\))[6](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/research/perspective/waterhouse-and-the-nymphs).

4. **Lucas Cranach the Elder - _The Nymph at the Fountain_**: Although not explicitly erotic, this painting features a sensual depiction of a nymph, combining classical references with a sensual charge, reflecting the artist's style of emphasizing sensuality in his female figures[3](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/cranach-lucas-elder/nymph-fountain).

These works illustrate how nymphs have been used in art to explore themes of desire, sensuality, and eroticism across different periods and styles.
