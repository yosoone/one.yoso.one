notion of sacred
[[identity]]
[[Japan]]

To me, public space is sacred, yes, but so is ‘the right to privacy’ or sanctum sanctorum, the issue is if we make it plainly political or simply functional. This reflects on my appreciation for life, so I can not make the case for everyone. Sometimes I am tempted to say that there is an almost subconscious state of sacred ingrained into Japanese culture, but this is a topic I need to get deeper into, and this reality seems to be shifting fast into 'meaningless consumerism'. For now, I would summarise it as a respect for intimacy or simply trying to maintain harmony. This could be one of the reasons for discomfort. There is also a complex relationship with the sense of self, as the role one plays in society is deeply ingrained and can be alienating if one confronts the norms or struggles with identity.

We are not moving away from language, our use of language reflects our ‘functioning, look into ‘kotodama’ if you are interested.

Maybe the grammatical point was, ’to feel in the state of… being sad, as opposed to saying, you are sad…' as mentioned in the article...

“Instead, it is required to explicitly indicate that the statement is based on an inference by the use of a marking suffix such as -garu (e.g., “Anata \[or Kare] ga kanashi garu kara \[because you (he) feel(s) sad])” (Wice et al., 2020, p. 2),

ironically we seem to loop back onto territorial...

“The most well-established interpretation among Japanese linguists concerning this constraint is that referring to others’ inner states, such as feelings and experiences, violates the norm of politeness (Brown & Levinson, 1987) and hence causes a sense of territorial invasion (e.g., Masuoka, 1997)” (Wice et al., 2020, p. 320)

Don’t quote me on this ;) Maybe one day!

I do not think people in Japan are against communities, especially in the countryside, people rely on each other, and there are still numerous festivals and seasonal celebrations. It is true, however, that some might try to escape the 'group identity', escaping the responsibilities involved, maybe finding anonymity in the bigger cities.
