# Kūkai, the Letter A, and the Dragon Girl — Main Points

Full note with sources: [[Kukai, the Letter A, and the Dragon Girl]].

## The world is scripture

- For Kūkai, Dainichi Nyorai (大日如来) _is_ the world. The Dharma-body preaches through everything that exists (_hosshin seppō_, 法身説法).[^1]
- His couplet 山毫点溟墨　乾坤経籍箱: mountains are the brush, the dark sea is the ink, and heaven and earth are the case that holds the sutras.[^2]

## The _Shōji jissō gi_ verse

- 五大皆有響: the five elements all resonate.
- 十界具言語: all ten realms speak.
- 六塵悉文字: everything sensed is a letter.
- 法身是実相: the Dharma-body _is_ reality.
- Visible form (colour, shape, movement) is read as script. This is the root of the brush-and-ink image.[^3]

## The letter A

- _A_ is the first sound of the open mouth, and it sits inside every Sanskrit syllable.
- It means _ādyanutpāda_ (阿字本不生), "originally unborn": emptiness, not a first cause.[^4]
- The one syllable holds the whole verse. Its sound is 声, its letter 字, its meaning 実相.
- _Ajikan_ (阿字観) is meditation on this letter.

## Can animals be buddhas?

- **Early Buddhism:** not while in an animal birth. Animals cannot be ordained.[^5]
- **Mahāyāna:** all beings have Buddha-nature.[^6]
- **Japan:** even grasses, trees and lands become Buddha (草木国土悉皆成仏).[^7]
- **Kūkai:** an animal is already a letter of the Dharma-body. What it lacks is recognition, not buddhahood.

## The dragon girl — _Lotus Sutra_, chapter 12

- An eight-year-old nāga girl awakens in an instant, offers a jewel, becomes male, and attains buddhahood in the south.[^8]
- The objection she overcomes is to her _female_ body. The tradition read the story as being about gender and sudden awakening, not animal birth.
- The chapter was a later addition to Kumārajīva's translation.[^9]
