The name 

Sabine has a Latin origin, meaning "==a woman of the Sabine people"==. The Sabines were an ancient Italic tribe who lived in central Italy before the founding of Rome. 

Ancient origins and Roman connection

- **The Sabine people:** The name is a direct reference to the Sabines, known in ancient Rome for their culture and religious traditions.
- **Historical legend:** According to legend, the early Romans, to populate their new city, abducted women from the Sabine tribe. After a war, the Sabines and Romans eventually merged into a single community.
- **A name for unity:** The legend means the name is associated with the themes of cultural fusion, strength, and resilience.
- **Variations:** The Latin form of the name is Sabina. 

Modern usage and revival

- **French and German popularity:** The personal name Sabine did not become widespread until centuries later. It gained particular popularity in the 1800s in parts of Europe, notably France and Germany.
- **Contemporary usage:** Today, the name is popular among German women and has a distinct European feel.
- **Variations:** The name has variants in other languages, such as Sabina in Italian and Spanish.
