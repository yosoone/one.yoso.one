# Token Culture — Research Notes

_Compiled from conversation — Soul Carver Archive_

---

## The Academic World

### Foundational Text — Rosabeth Moss Kanter (1977)

_Men and Women of the Corporation_ is the cornerstone of academic thinking on token culture. Kanter concluded that "tokens" — members of a subgroup representing less than 15% of the overall group — are likely to have negative workplace experiences as a result of their low numerical representation.

Three pressures on tokens identified:

- **Visibility** — their differences are magnified
- **Contrast** — the dominant group exaggerates its own culture in response
- **Assimilation** — tokens are forced into stereotyped roles

Tokens may fight stereotypes by asserting individuality, or assimilate — but when they assimilate, stereotypes are actually perpetuated further.

---

### Judith Long Laws — The Sponsor Relationship

Tokenism is built on a mutually agreed-upon but deceptive relationship between the token and a sponsor. The token gains limited access in exchange for not challenging the system, while the institution maintains the appearance of progress.

---

### Sociology — The Basic Definition

Tokenism is the practice of making a perfunctory, symbolic effort at inclusion of minority groups — particularly by recruiting from underrepresented groups so the organisation gives the _appearance_ of racial and gender equality, without substantive cultural change.

---

### Epistemic Exclusion

Research on faculty of colour identified _epistemic exclusion_ — where formal institutional evaluation systems combine with individual biases to devalue minority scholars' work and deem them illegitimate. This occurs both through formal hierarchies and informal processes that signal to faculty of colour that they and their scholarship are devalued.

---

### Racialized Tokens

The term "racialized tokens" illustrates how race, gender, and class combine to shape the workplace experiences of minority workers who exist as numerical minorities among majority-white colleagues — a dynamic that goes beyond Kanter's original gender-neutral framework.

---

### Cultural Foundations

Research in the _American Sociological Review_ challenged earlier models by arguing that cultural context dramatically shapes how tokenism plays out differently across industries and settings.

---

## The Spiritual and Theological World

### Christian and Religious Justice Frameworks

Malcolm X's declaration that _"tokenism is hypocrisy"_ is echoed in religious anti-racism frameworks, which argue that tokenism merely _tolerates_ difference rather than genuinely including it. At worst, it is described as an illusion — a false narrative about commitment to justice. Intentions do not nullify the outcome of tokenism.

---

### Womanist and Black Feminist Theology

Drawing on Audre Lorde's insight that _"diversity without structural change is tokenism"_, womanist theologians and Black feminist critics argue that representation without radical political analysis amounts to mere assimilation — and that true equity requires revolting against the status quo, not managing appearances within it.

---

### The Performative Virtue Critique

Musa al-Gharbi's _We Have Never Been Woke_ applies Nietzschean and Foucauldian analysis to social justice movements, arguing that "symbolic capitalists" — elite professionals at the forefront of justice language — often act in ways that exacerbate inequality. The book closes with Jesus's words from Matthew 6 warning against _performative virtue_: if true justice demands integrity, the loudest voices should first examine their quietest actions.

---

### Buddhist Perspective

Teachings on emptiness, compassion, and universal liberation "risk becoming yet another brand of spiritual ornamentation for a privileged class" — warning that even contemplative traditions can be co-opted into performative social justice advocacy that substitutes symbol for substance.

---

### Decolonising Spiritual Inclusion

Recent work in the _Encyclopedia of Diversity, Equity, Inclusion and Spirituality_ focuses on moving beyond performative inclusion toward justice-anchored cultures that cultivate pluralism, healing, and interdependence.

---

## AI Tokens — A Different Thing Entirely

The word "token" in AI is a purely technical concept — the basic unit of text that a language model processes. Roughly a word or word-fragment. The word comes from _lexical tokenisation_ in linguistics and computer science, used since the 1960s/70s.

**The distinction:**

- **AI token** = a unit of text data
- **Social token** = a symbolic/performative gesture

Same word. Entirely different lineages. No intellectual or historical connection — though both involve something being _reduced to a unit_ that stands in for something larger and more complex.

---

## The Common Thread

Across all fields — organisational sociology, critical race theory, feminist theology, Buddhist ethics — the central critique is consistent:

> Token culture substitutes _symbolic presence_ for _structural change_, and in doing so actually stabilises the systems it appears to challenge.

The token functions as proof that the system is working, precisely so the system doesn't have to change.

---

## Related Observation — Technology Waves

The same pattern repeats across history:

- **Industrial revolution** — workers got safety valves (unions, regulations) but capital retained fundamental control
- **Internet** — users got voices (comments, likes) but algorithms and ad revenue shaped everything
- **Social media** — "your profile, your network" but data, attention, profit flowed upward
- **AI** — "your assistant, your tool" but training data, values, infrastructure belong to a handful of companies

Each wave offers _participation_ without _power_. A sophisticated form of the same token dynamic — symbolic inclusion that stabilises the underlying hierarchy.
