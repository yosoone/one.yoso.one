See [[Copenhagen]]

It’s considered normal for cities and states to measure success in terms of economic growth. But [critics](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2427.2012.01167.x) point to the treadmill of addictive consumption, property speculation, long working hours, debt, waste, one-upmanship, fast food and short-lifespan technologies that unending growth sets in motion. Opposing this trend, communities such as Christiania pursue “[degrowth](https://eprint.ncl.ac.uk/228687)” by prioritising human relations over market relations; maximising sharing, togetherness, social justice and the health of the planet.
