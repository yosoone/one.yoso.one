# Claude Ref

The liver is actually one of the earliest and most active organs in fetal development — and it does something unexpected for most of pregnancy.

**Week 3:** The liver appears at about the third week of gestation, and grows rapidly from weeks 5 to 10 under WNT/β-Catenin signaling, which induces hepatic progenitor cells to proliferate and differentiate into hepatocytes.

**Week 5:** It begins forming alongside the stomach and pancreas, and is among the first abdominal organs to emerge.

**Week 6 — blood factory:** This is the liver's most surprising early role. By week 6, the liver is already producing red blood cells — long before bone marrow takes over that job. Early in gestation the liver is the primary site for hematopoiesis, and at seven weeks, hematopoietic cells (blood-forming cells) actually outnumber hepatocytes (liver cells).

**Week 8–10:** By week 8, the liver begins to function. By week 10, the kidneys, liver, brain, and lungs are now actually functioning.

**Week 11:** Red blood cells are beginning to form in the liver, and this hematopoietic role continues through mid-pregnancy.

**Week 12:** The liver produces bile, and the circulatory, digestive, and urinary systems are working.

**Week 14+:** The liver and spleen continue developing more during the second trimester.

**Peak size — week 9:** The liver attains its highest relative size at about 10% of fetal weight at the ninth week of gestation. It's proportionally enormous compared to the adult liver, which makes up only about 2% of body weight.

**Ongoing maturation:** As the fetus nears term, hepatocytes predominate and enlarge with expansion of the endoplasmic reticulum and accumulation of glycogen. Hepatic blood flow, plasma protein binding, and intrinsic clearance by the liver undergo significant postnatal maturation — the final steps of biochemical differentiation, including synthesis of enzymes to process solid food, occur just after birth.
