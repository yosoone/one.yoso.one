# Silicon Valley Drug Use Statistics -claude

> _“We are obsessed with drugs here.”_
> — Jhonny, San Francisco tech worker, \$300k/year

---

## Overview

There is no single definitive figure, but data from surveys, rehab centers, and investigative reporting paints a clear picture: Silicon Valley has significantly higher drug use rates than most industries — and much of it happens _during work_, not just after hours.

---

## By Substance

|Substance                                |Silicon Valley Rate|National Average|
|-----------------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
|Prescription stimulants (non-medical)    |~34%               |~18%            |
|Adderall during crunch periods           |~28%               |—               |
|Binge drinking                           |~42%               |~24% (CA avg)   |
|General mind-altering substances for work|~15–20%            |—               |

---

## Breakdown

### Prescription Stimulants (Adderall, Ritalin)

34% of tech workers have used prescription stimulants non-medically to enhance performance. Adderall use is particularly common among software engineers, with 28% reporting regular use during crunch periods or challenging projects.

### Psychedelics & Microdosing

Microdosing — taking minimal regular doses of LSD or psilocybin — has become one of the most openly discussed drug trends in tech. Workers claim it boosts creativity, focus, and productivity.

### Cocaine

In some work environments, cocaine use has moved beyond an open secret to being openly normalized. High incomes give tech workers direct access to high-grade supply, with dealers reportedly servicing tech campuses on demand.

### Alcohol

\~42% of tech professionals engage in binge drinking — nearly double California’s average of 24%. Networking events, startup celebrations, and work-related social activities drive elevated rates.

---

## Why So High?

- **Extreme work pressure** — 80-hour weeks and constant deadlines drive stimulant use
- **High disposable income** — workers can afford premium and illegal substances
- **Normalization from the top** — Steve Jobs, Bill Gates, and others have alluded to drug use
- **Lax enforcement** — many companies have a “don’t ask, don’t tell” policy and don’t regularly test employees
- **Performance culture** — drugs are framed as productivity tools, not recreational vices

---

## Caveats

These numbers come largely from self-reported surveys, rehab centers, and journalists — not rigorous population studies. Real figures are hard to pin down because much of the use is illegal and hidden.

> **Bottom line:** Significantly more than most industries, and likely more than is publicly acknowledged.
