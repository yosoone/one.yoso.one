**ψ**

## Use as a symbol

The letter psi is commonly used in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") to represent [wave functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function "Wave function") in [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), such as in the [Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schr%C3%B6dinger_equation "Schrödinger equation") and [bra–ket notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bra%E2%80%93ket_notation "Bra–ket notation"): ⟨ϕ|ψ⟩![{\displaystyle \langle \phi |\psi \rangle }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7d9869dca1c0dd01660954d2214ed16ca6115faf). It is also used to represent the (generalized) positional states of a [qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit "Qubit") in a [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer "Quantum computer").

Psi is also used as the symbol for the [polygamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygamma_function "Polygamma function"), defined by

ψ(m)(z)=dmdzmΓ′(z)Γ(z)![{\displaystyle \psi ^{(m)}(z)={\frac {d^{m}}{dz^{m}}}{\frac {\Gamma '(z)}{\Gamma (z)}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/1decc04e85e32475f823041cd37367c7133f0b1b)

where Γ(z)![{\displaystyle \Gamma (z)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/11ca17f880240539116aac7e6326909299e2a080) is the [gamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_function "Gamma function").[[3]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-3)

The letters Ψ or ψ can also be a symbol for:

- [Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"), [psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatry "Psychiatry"), and sometimes [parapsychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapsychology "Parapsychology")[[4]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-4)(involving [paranormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranormal "Paranormal") or relating with the [supernatural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural")subjects, especially research into extrasensory perception).
- In mathematics, the [reciprocal Fibonacci constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_Fibonacci_constant "Reciprocal Fibonacci constant"),[[5]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-5) the [division polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_polynomials "Division polynomials"), and the [supergolden ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergolden_ratio "Supergolden ratio").
- In mathematics, the second [Chebyshev function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_function "Chebyshev function")[[6]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-6)
- [Water potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_potential "Water potential") in movement of water between plant cells.[[7]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-7)
- In [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"), it denotes [pseudouridine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudouridine "Pseudouridine"), an uncommon [nucleoside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoside "Nucleoside").[[8]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-8)
- [Stream function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_function "Stream function") in [fluid mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_mechanics "Fluid mechanics") defining the curve to which the [flow velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity "Flow velocity") is always [tangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent "Tangent").
- One of the [dihedral angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_angle "Dihedral angle") in the backbones of [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteins "Proteins").[[9]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-9)
- [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University "Indiana University") (as a superimposed [I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I "I") and [U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U "U")).[[10]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-10)
- A [sai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_\(weapon\) "Sai (weapon)"), the name of which is pronounced the same way in English.
- [Pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology "Pharmacology"), general [pharmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacy "Pharmacy").
- In [virology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virology "Virology") the ψ site is a viral packaging signal.[[11]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-11)
- The [J/ψ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J/%CF%88 "J/ψ") meson, in [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics").[[12]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-12)
- In the [computability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computability_theory "Computability theory"), ψP(x)![{\displaystyle \psi \_{P}(x),!}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0b3ac9acfee19a305644fff4cb3e828d531e6c76) represents the return value Y![{\displaystyle Y,!}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/82e65066132416db15d76f148ea4436daf49c863) of a program P![{\displaystyle P,!}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7c3e0ad16487ef34cb49cae8db35e4333ec4d491).
- In [circadian physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circadian_physiology "Circadian physiology"), ψ represents the phase relationship between a [zeitgeber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitgeber "Zeitgeber") and a [biological rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_rhythm "Biological rhythm").[[13]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-13)
- In [building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building "Building"), to represent an adjustment to a [U-value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-value "U-value"), accounting for [thermal bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_bridge "Thermal bridge") effects.
- The [ordinal collapsing function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_collapsing_function "Ordinal collapsing function") and [notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_notation "Ordinal notation") developed by Wilfried Buchholz.[[14]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-14)
- In [Biblical studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_studies "Biblical studies"), as an abbreviation for the book of [Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms "Psalms").[[15]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi\_(Greek)#cite\_note-15)
