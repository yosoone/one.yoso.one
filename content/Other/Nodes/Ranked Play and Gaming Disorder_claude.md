# Ranked Play and Gaming Disorder — An Academic Review

_Source: Claude, Anthropic, Claude Opus 5, conversation with author, 11 September 2026. Scholarly claims rest on the peer-reviewed and primary sources in the footnotes._

## Nosology — Classification

The eleventh revision of the International Classification of Diseases defines gaming disorder (6C51) through three core features: impaired control over gaming, rising priority given to gaming above other interests and daily activities, and continuation or escalation of play in the face of negative consequences, with significant functional impairment normally evident across at least 12 months.[^1] The diagnosis sits within the category "Disorders due to addictive behaviours," a lineage traced to Isaac Marks's concept of behavioural addictions, and the duration requirement may shorten to six months in severe presentations.[^2] The DSM-5 takes a more provisional position, placing Internet Gaming Disorder in Section III as a condition for further study.[^3]

The field holds two positions side by side. Aarseth and colleagues identified a low-quality research base, criteria modelled closely on substance use and gambling, and a risk of moral panic producing abundant false-positive diagnoses among young people.[^4] Higuchi and colleagues at Kurihama answered from clinical data, reporting that most treatment seekers at their specialist service were engaged with online games and frequently presented with severe symptoms.[^5]

## Epidemiology — Prevalence

Stevens, Dorstyn, Delfabbro, and King pooled a worldwide prevalence of 3.05%, adjusting to 1.96% for studies meeting stricter sampling criteria.[^6] A corrigendum removed two studies, revising the pooled estimate to 2.96% and reporting males as 2.3 times more likely to meet criteria.[^7]

## Aetiology — The I-PACE Model

Brand and colleagues' Interaction of Person-Affect-Cognition-Execution model offers the dominant framework for how addictive use of specific applications, gaming included, develops and persists, with ventral striatal and prefrontal function and conditioning processes shaping the cycle.[^8] The 2019 update describes addictive behaviour as the product of interactions among predisposing variables, affective and cognitive responses to specific cues, and executive functions, and proposes that compensatory relief grows stronger than gratification in later stages.[^9] This developmental claim bears directly on ranked play, since it predicts a movement from playing for reward toward playing for relief.

## Structure — What the Ladder Adds

Ranked modes attach a cumulative ledger to each match. In League of Legends, ranked matches carry league points that accumulate into a ladder standing, and participants in a recent study rated ranked play as markedly more competitive than the casual ARAM mode.[^10] ==Qualitative research with League players found that each loss asks for an additional win== simply to hold position, giving single matches considerable weight, and some players described anxiety arising while entering the ranked queue.[^11]

Matchmaking forms a second design lever. Chen and colleagues observed that conventional systems pair similarly skilled players on the assumption that fair games serve players best, and proposed Engagement Optimized Matchmaking, which pairs players to minimise predicted churn and treats equal-skill matching as a special case holding under simplified assumptions; they tested the framework in simulation on 36.9 million one-versus-one matches from 1.68 million players of an Electronic Arts title.[^12] The paper establishes that ==match outcomes can be engineered toward retention==; its presence in live ranked systems awaits public documentation.

Flayelle, Andronicos, King, and Billieux call for interactionist research into how specific design features align with player motivations, identifying achievement among the motives more strongly associated with problematic gaming.[^13]

## Motivation — The Differentiating Variable

Across large samples, motivation distinguishes risk more clearly than hours played. Larrieu, Billieux, and Décamps identified ==three motivational clusters among online competitive players: recreational, competitive, and escapers==.[^14] A prospective follow-up with objective playtime data reproduced the clusters, locating engaged and well-functioning players in the competitive group and vulnerability among escapers; escape motivation drove the partition most strongly, and the groups logged equivalent playtime, marking hours as a weak indicator of harm.[^15]

Bányai, Griffiths, Demetrovics, and Király found that esports players spent more time gaming on weekdays and weekends than recreational players and scored higher on social, competitive, and skill-development motives.[^16] Mangat and colleagues extended this with a three-group comparison: esports players showed negative affectivity within a healthy range and were driven by competition and social connection, while highly engaged gamers showed high negative affectivity and low sociability and were driven by escapism and mastery.[^17]

The convergent reading holds that competitive motivation alone predicts engagement and wellbeing, while competition joined with escape predicts risk, consistent with the compensatory shift described in the I-PACE update. Self-determination research aligns with this, linking obsessive engagement with games to frustrated psychological needs in daily life.[^18]

## Affect — Tilt

Bonk and Tamminen describe tilt as a cycle in which performance failures produce negative emotion that further degrades play, sometimes culminating in ragequitting, an abrupt, frustration-driven exit from competition.[^19] Among 1,007 gamers, Cregan and colleagues found tilt risk rising with competitive motivation, anger, and hours played, and protection arising from years of experience and adaptive emotion regulation.[^20] League esports players interviewed in 2025 managed tilt by muting chat, redirecting attention, accepting and releasing the triggering event, and taking breaks.[^21] Poker research establishes the gambling lineage: among 291 online poker players, tilt frequency and cognitive distortion predicted excessive gambling, with tilt showing the stronger association.[^22]

## Neurobiology — Reward and Control

Koepp and colleagues' positron emission tomography study demonstrated endogenous dopamine release in the human striatum during video game play, with release tracking performance and concentrating in the ventral striatum.[^23] Weinstein's review of imaging research reports that Internet Gaming Disorder shares neurobiological alterations with other addictions, including heightened reward-region activation and diminished functional connectivity in networks governing cognitive control, executive function, motivation, and reward.[^24]

## Buddhist Psychology — The Scholarly Literature

Groves and Farmer argued in _Addiction Research_ that Buddhist doctrine analyses craving and attachment in detail — their arising, forms, results, and management — and that this analysis, together with the teaching on impermanence, speaks to both the causation and the treatment of addiction, framing their account through the Four Noble Truths.[^25] Marlatt drew on the same work to describe addictive objects as false refuges taken through ignorance and met, in Buddhist practice, with compassion.[^26]

Three primary texts illuminate ranked structure. The Soṇa Sutta examines self-measurement as better, equal, or worse (_seyyo'ham asmi_, _sadiso'ham asmi_, _hīno'ham asmi_) on the basis of aggregates subject to change, and names release from all three measures as seeing truly.[^27] ==The Sallatha Sutta contrasts the worldling, who meets painful feeling with added mental anguish like a second arrow following the first, with the noble disciple, who feels the first arrow alone.==[^28] The Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta describes craving as leading to renewed becoming and as seeking delight now here, now there.[^29] Reading rank tiers, tilt, and repeated queueing through these three texts is an interpretive bridge offered by this note.

## Treatment — Contemplative Intervention

Li and colleagues randomised thirty adults to eight weeks of Mindfulness-Oriented Recovery Enhancement or a support group; the mindfulness group achieved greater reductions in DSM-5 criteria, craving, and maladaptive gaming-related cognitions, with benefits maintained at three months.[^30] A mediation analysis linked these gains to reduced maladaptive gaming cognitions and increased positive reappraisal.[^31]

## Japan — Clinical Context

Kurihama Medical and Addiction Center (久里浜医療センター) in Kanagawa opened Japan's first internet addiction programme in 2011; by 2019, 84 programmes operated nationwide, and 241 of the centre's 269 internet addiction patients held gaming disorder as their primary addiction.[^32] Kitayuguchi and colleagues trace how specialist treatment delivery in Japan has since evolved.[^33]

## Fidelity Notes

Ranked-specific evidence draws predominantly from League of Legends, so extension to other ladder systems remains inference. The EOMM simulation concerns a one-versus-one title, and its application to team-based ranked play is extrapolation. The 2022 and 2023 Larrieu abstracts circulate with overlapping text in secondary indexes and warrant verification against the published articles. Koepp's 1998 study used a very small sample. The MORE trial is a small Stage I study, and its developer, Eric Garland, receives income from the treatment manual and trainings. The Kurihama figures come from a 2019 feature in the _Bulletin of the World Health Organization_. Author lists for footnotes 2, 10, 11, 21, and 22 and the publication year for footnote 19 await confirmation against the published records. The Buddhist parallels are interpretive; the suttas address conceit, feeling, and craving in general terms.
