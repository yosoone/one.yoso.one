# The Hungry Battle Ghost — A Ranked Match in Six Bardos

_Source: Claude, Anthropic, Claude Opus 5, conversation with author, 11 September 2026. Traditional and scholarly claims rest on the primary and academic sources in the footnotes._

## Frame — Realms as Moments

Chögyam Trungpa taught bardo as the peak experience of any given moment, a moment always coloured by one of six psychological realms; he glossed the hungry ghost realm as poverty and possessiveness, and the jealous god realm as jealousy.[^1] He located these realms within ordinary life, naming the domestic hungers of the hungry ghosts alongside the competitive problem of the asuras.[^2] The piece below borrows his method, reading one ranked match of League of Legends as a passage through the six bardos of the Karma Lingpa cycle.[^3]

Japanese iconography supplies the ghost's body. The Kyoto National Museum's 『餓鬼草紙』(_Gaki zōshi_, Scroll of Hungry Ghosts), from the late Heian period, depicts the hungry ghost realm within the six paths of transmigration, _rokudō_ (六道, six realms), including thirst-tormented ghosts among 36 kinds, and a flame-belching ghost released through an incantation Ānanda received from Shakyamuni — the origin of _segaki_ (施餓鬼, feeding the hungry ghosts).[^4] The Tokyo National Museum notes that six-realm imagery spread through Genshin's 『往生要集』(_Ōjōyōshū_, Essentials of Rebirth in the Pure Land), and that the greedy, the selfish, and the jealous fall into the ghost realm together.[^5]

That shared doorway gives birth to the battle ghost: a preta's hunger wearing asura armour.

## The Match

### I. Kyé-né — The Bardo of This Life

The queue pops with a sound like a temple bell struck by a child.

Child of the Rift, listen. You are born at the fountain, in the lit corner of the map where every life on this field begins, holding a little gold and a full bar of health. This is the bardo of this life, the span between birth and death, and it lasts as long as a match lasts: thirty minutes, forty, the length of a meal eaten standing.

Beside you, visible to you alone, the battle ghost wakes. Its belly is vast, a sack that could hold every league point ever won; its throat is narrow as the eye of a needle.[^6] Over its ribs it wears lacquered asura armour cracked at the seams, and in its hand it carries a blade notched from wars fought over a tree.

The ghost knows the tree well. Its roots drink from the ghost's own soil; its fruit ripens far above, in the realm of the gods, golden and crested.[^7] The ghost has spent lifetimes climbing toward that fruit. It speaks one word as you walk toward lane, and the word is a number.

### II. Milam — The Bardo of Dream

The middle of the match arrives the way sleep arrives, all at once, with the edges softened. Minions break against each other in waves. You farm, you rotate, you take a dragon, and time folds into the rhythm of last hits. This is the bardo of dream: a world vivid and wholly convincing, built from the mind's own material.

In the dream the ghost grows tender. It shows you the self you will be three divisions higher: calm, respected, complete. It feeds you the image in small spoonfuls, and each spoonful passes down the needle throat as a single drop, and the belly aches for the next.

Somewhere in the house around your body, tea cools. The window turns from blue to black. The dream holds.

### III. Samten — The Bardo of Meditation

Between fights, a gap opens. You stand in a brush at the river's edge, waiting for an enemy who may arrive. The ward glows. Your hand rests on the mouse. The breath, shallow for twenty minutes, drops into the belly and settles there.

This is the bardo of meditation, and it appears in the middle of war the way a clearing appears in a forest. For three seconds you see the whole arrangement: the hand, the screen, the ghost at your shoulder with its armour, its hunger, the number it keeps repeating. You see that the ghost is made of attention, and that the attention is yours.

The ghost goes very still, the way animals go still once they are seen. Then the enemy steps into the brush, the gap closes, and the fight begins.

### IV. Chikhai — The Bardo of Dying

You die. The screen drains of colour: grey trees, grey river, grey allies moving through a world open to your eyes and closed to your hands. A timer counts in the centre. The tradition describes the dissolutions of dying element by element — earth into water, water into fire, fire into wind — until awareness rests in a clear light brighter than anything the living eye has met.[^8]

Here the ghost does its most intimate work, filling the grey silence with a second arrow.[^9] The first arrow was the death itself: a misjudged step, a spell arriving from the fog. The second is the sentence the ghost writes across the grey — the teammate's fault, the matchmaker's cruelty, the points already falling. The first arrow lasts the length of a timer. The second can last all night.

The timer ends. You are reborn at the fountain and walk back toward the fight carrying both arrows.

Forty minutes in, the enemy breaks the base. The Nexus cracks and opens outward in a slow white burst, and for one moment the entire screen is light.

### V. Chönyi — The Bardo of Luminosity

DEFEAT.

Then the numbers arrive: kills, deaths, assists, damage dealt, vision score, and glowing at the centre, the league points taken away. This is the bardo of dharmata, where the peaceful and wrathful deities blaze up in brilliant colour, and the text instructs the one who sees them to recognise every terrifying form as the radiance of their own mind.[^10]

The statistics screen is a mandala of wrathful deities. Each figure blazes with its own light: the death count flame-red, the damage graph a tower of blue, the point loss a single number wreathed in fire. The ghost bows before them in terror and hunger both, for these are the gods it serves.

Child of the Rift, recognise them. Every number on this screen arose from the same attention that watched the brush and felt the breath. They shine because you lit them.

### VI. Sidpa — The Bardo of Becoming

Now the lobby: the chat window, the honour vote, and in the lower corner a button reading PLAY AGAIN. This is the bardo of becoming, where the mind, carried on karmic wind, drifts toward a new birth, and the lights of the six realms glow before it like doorways, each soft and inviting. The text gives its instruction plainly: close the entrance to the womb.[^11]

The ghost's belly is emptier now than it has ever been. It has lost points, and its hunger stands at full flood. It lays its notched blade against your wrist and guides the cursor toward the button with great gentleness. One more returns what was taken. One more climbs the tree.

The lights shine from the lobby. The pale white light of the gods, where the last victory still glows. The red light of the jealous gods, where the enemy's crest burns. The yellow light of the ghosts, colour of an empty bowl. You feel the pull of each.

Before you choose a door, you honour a teammate — the support who warded all game and died twice keeping you alive. It takes one click. It is a small act of _muditā_, gladness at another's good, and the ghost's needle throat opens a little wider to receive it.

### Coda — Segaki

The client closes. The room returns: cold tea, black window, a body stiff in the chair. You fill a cup with water, stand at the window, and pour a little into the soil of a potted plant — the gesture of the mourners in the Kyoto scroll, who pour water over a grave marker for their parents while thin figures gather to drink.[^12]

This is segaki, the feeding of the hungry ghosts. The ghost drinks. For the length of a breath its belly softens, its armour loosens at the shoulder, and its face becomes the face of someone who once loved this game on a summer evening, laughing with friends.

Child of the Rift, the ghost is fed by what is given. Tomorrow the queue will ring its little bell again. Whichever door you choose, choose it awake.

## Analysis — Reading the Match

**The ledger.** The ranked ladder renders the preta's proportions in arithmetic. In League of Legends, ranked matches carry league points that accumulate into a ladder standing,[^13] and players describe each loss asking for an extra win simply to hold position.[^14] Gains arrive a drop at a time through the needle throat; one defeat empties the bowl in a single pour.

**Hunger and war together.** The Tokyo National Museum's gloss of scripture places the greedy and the jealous side by side in the ghost realm,[^5] while Trungpa distinguishes the hungers of the ghosts from the competitive problem of the jealous gods.[^2] The battle ghost stands where these two accounts meet, and the asura war over the wish-fulfilling tree — rooted in the asura realm, fruiting among the gods — gives the ladder its mythic shape.[^7]

**The second arrow.** The grey death screen stages Bonk and Tamminen's tilt cycle, in which failure breeds negative emotion, emotion degrades play, and the sequence can end in ragequitting.[^15] The Sallatha Sutta describes the same structure: the worldling meets painful feeling with added mental anguish, a second arrow following the first.[^9]

**The diet of relief.** The ghost's hunger peaks after defeat. Brand and colleagues' updated I-PACE model proposes that compensatory relief comes to outweigh gratification in later stages of addictive behaviour,[^16] and escape motivation marks the vulnerable profile among competitive players.[^17] Loss enlarges the relief the ghost seeks.

**The womb door.** The root instruction of the bardo of becoming directs the practitioner to close the entrance to the womb.[^11] The Play Again button occupies that doorway.

**The offering.** The Kyoto scroll depicts water poured for deceased parents drawing thirsty ghosts,[^12] and traces the segaki rite to Ānanda passing the method of salvation to the monks.[^4] The Pali parallel appears in the Tirokudda Kanda, where petas find relief through merit dedicated by living relatives[^18] — see [[Buddhism/Concepts/Merit|Merit]].

## Fidelity Notes

Reading the six bardos as moments within a match follows Trungpa's present-moment interpretation; the classical cycle addresses death and rebirth. Bardo names and realm-light colours vary across translations, with Dorje's complete translation and the Fremantle–Trungpa edition giving the fuller textual record. The battle ghost is an invented figure fusing preta and asura iconography. The needle-eyed throat and the war over the wish-fulfilling tree follow Patrul Rinpoche's classical presentation of the realms. The Kyoto and Tokyo _Gaki zōshi_ are distinct works. The rendering 焔口 (_engu_) for the flame-mouthed ghost awaits confirmation against the museum's Japanese catalogue before inclusion.
