Yama and Niyama

These are the first two of the eight limbs of yoga, as outlined by the sage Patanjali in the Yoga Sutras around 150 BCE. The yamas are things not to do — restraints — while the niyamas are things to do — observances. Together they form a moral code of conduct. ￼

The yamas primarily focus on our actions when in community with others, while the niyamas focus more on our relationship with our physical and psychological selves. ￼

The Five Yamas (Social Ethics)

```
1.	Ahimsa – Non-violence: the principle of being non-harming in thought, word, and deed. ￼
2.	Satya – Truthfulness: commitment to honesty in speech and action.
3.	Asteya – Non-stealing: refraining from taking what does not belong to you.
4.	Brahmacharya – Abstinence ￼; often interpreted more broadly as right use of energy.
5.	Aparigraha – Non-accumulation or non-greed; letting go of excess.
```

The Five Niyamas (Personal Observances)

```
1.	Saucha – Internal and external cleanliness. ￼
2.	Santosha – Being happy and content. ￼
3.	Tapas – Penance ￼, or disciplined effort and self-discipline.
4.	Svadhyaya – Self-study ￼; reflection and study of sacred texts.
5.	Ishvara Pranidhana – Surrender to a higher power. ￼
```

Purpose

The yamas are about restraining behaviors motivated by grasping, aversion, hatred, and delusion. The niyamas are designed to create well-being for ourselves and others. ￼ They function as a kind of compass — showing when we are aligned with our practice and when we are off course. ￼
