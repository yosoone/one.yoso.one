## Hylomorphism

(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylomorphism)

**Hylomorphism** is a [philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy") doctrine developed by the [Ancient Greek philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_philosophy "Ancient Greek philosophy") [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle"), which conceives every physical entity or [being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being "Being") (_[ousia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ousia "Ousia")_) as a compound of [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter "Matter") (potency) and [immaterial form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantial_form "Substantial form") (act), with the generic form as [immanently real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderate_realism "Moderate realism") within the individual.[[1]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylomorphism#cite\_note-Strauss\_2014-1) The word is a 19th-century term formed from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language "Ancient Greek language") words ὕλη (_hyle_: "wood, matter") and μορφή (_morphē_: "form").[[1]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylomorphism#cite\_note-Strauss\_2014-1) Hylomorphic theories of physical entities have been undergoing a revival in contemporary philosophy.[[250324]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylomorphism#cite\_note-2)
