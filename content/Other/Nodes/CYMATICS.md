Cymatics is a field that combines the study of visible effects of sound and vibration with the creation of art. It involves the use of various techniques to visualize sound waves, often in the form of patterns or shapes, which can be used as a basis for artistic expression. The term "cymatics" comes from the Ancient Greek word _κῦμα_ (kuma), meaning "wave," and the suffix _-ics_, which denotes a study or branch of knowledge.

In the context of art, cymatics can be used to create visual representations of sound, often by using materials like sand or salt on a vibrating surface, such as a plate or speaker. These materials respond to the vibrations of the sound waves, creating intricate patterns that can be captured and displayed as art. This combination of sound and visual art can be particularly captivating, as it allows viewers to see and experience the invisible world of sound in a tangible way.

Cymatics has been used in various artistic contexts, such as music performances, where the patterns produced by sound waves are synchronized with the music, creating a visually engaging experience for the audience. It has also been used in collaboration with other artists, such as in the case of Björk's world tour, where cymatic patterns were choreographed and synced to the basslines of songs from her album.

To create cymatic art, one can use a thin plate, a pinch of loose material like sand or salt, and produce sound waves in the center of the plate. The sound waves spread out from the center, causing the plate and the sand or salt to vibrate, creating intricate patterns. These patterns can be captured and displayed as art, offering a unique and visually stunning representation of sound.
