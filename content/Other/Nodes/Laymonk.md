# Gemini

Lay Buddhist practitioners, known as _upasakas_ (male) and _upasikas_(female), are ==followers who integrate the Buddha's teachings into their ordinary lives outside of a monastery==. They play a vital role in sustaining the wider Buddhist community by upholding ethical practices and supporting monks and nuns. 

Core aspects of lay practice

- **Taking refuge:** A lay Buddhist formally commits to the Three Jewels: the Buddha (the teacher), the Dharma (the teachings), and the Sangha (the community).
- **Following the Five Precepts:** These are fundamental ethical guidelines for laypeople:
  1. Refraining from killing or harming living beings.
  2. Refraining from taking what is not freely given.
  3. Refraining from sexual misconduct.
  4. Refraining from false speech.
  5. Refraining from intoxicating substances that cloud the mind.
- **Engaging in generosity (_dāna_):** Lay practitioners offer financial or material support to temples and monastics, often by providing alms (food, robes, medicine). This practice is a way to gain merit (good karma) and cultivate a mind free from stinginess.
- **Studying the Dharma:** Lay Buddhists listen to and study the teachings of the Buddha by attending Dharma talks, reading scriptures, and seeking guidance from teachers. 

Differences from monastic life

The primary distinction between lay and monastic life is the level of commitment to asceticism and daily discipline. 

|Aspect|Lay Practitioners|Monastic Practitioners (Monks & Nuns)|
|---|---|---|
|**Lifestyle**|Live domestic lives with family, careers, and property.|Renounce household life to devote themselves fully to spiritual practice.|
|**Precepts**|Typically follow the Five Precepts, with the option to observe more on special days.|Observe a much more extensive set of precepts, including complete celibacy.|
|**Daily Focus**|Balance spiritual practice with everyday responsibilities.|Dedicate all their time to study, meditation, and community service.|
|**Enlightenment**|Can attain high levels of spiritual development and, in some traditions, even enlightenment.|Generally understood to have a faster path to liberation due to their focused lifestyle.|

Daily practices of lay Buddhists

Lay practitioners integrate Buddhist principles into their daily activities, which can include: 

- **Home altars:** Many create a shrine at home with a Buddha statue, candles, and incense for personal reflection and worship.
- **Meditation:** Daily meditation is a common practice for cultivating mindfulness and wisdom.
- **Mindfulness:** Practitioners focus on being present in their daily tasks, such as mindfully tasting food or concentrating at work.
- **Kind actions:** Practicing generosity and kindness in daily interactions with family, colleagues, and strangers builds good karma.
- **Temple visits:** While not mandatory, visiting temples for prayer, offerings, and festivals is a popular practice.
