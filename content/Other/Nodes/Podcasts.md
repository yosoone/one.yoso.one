# BBC TIPS

Don't pay for anything. This is how BBC Radio does it: You connect with your guest via Zoom, Skype, Facetime or whatever via laptop or tablet. You both wear earbuds so that you can hear one another but no sound comes out of your laptop speakers at either end of the conversation. Next to you on your desk, place a smartphone on a pile of books so that the mic is about 30cm from your face. Start the phone recording using the voice memo function. Your guests does exactly the same - smartphone recording via voice memo on a stable surface. When you are sure both phones are recording, and when you can both hear one another clearly via Zoom, start your interview. When all questions have been asked and answered, stop the voice memo recordings. Your guest then shares his/her voice memo audio file with you via WeTransfer (or another file sharing platform). Import the guest audio into Audacity. Import your own voice memo audio into Audacity. These will be two separate tracks. Your track will be your questions and then periods of silence. Likewise, the guest's track will be answers interspersed with silence. The two tracks should be easily matched by sliding them from side to side using the drag function. If your files are in a format not recognised by Audacity, convert them to mp3 using [www.online-audio-converter.com](http://www.online-audio-converter.com/) Remember mp3 at a bit rate of 192 is fantastic for podcasting. Any bigger and your files will become unwieldy. Anyway, this method has been in use at the BBC and other networks for more than a decade and is known as a 'simulrec'. Audio recorded in situ, even on a smartphone, is almost always better quality than audio that's travelled around the world via fibre optic cable. Also, I'm guessing you already have everything you need to achieve this. And Audacity is both excellent and free.
