Baghavad p63

This is from Eknath Easwaran’s introduction to his Bhagavad Gita translation (Nilgiri Press), the section “Faith and Spiritual Evolution.”

Shraddha — that which is placed in the heart

Shraddha (श्रद्धा) is the concept Easwaran holds for last. Its nearest English equivalent is faith, yet it reaches deeper — literally “that which is placed in the heart”: the values, axioms, and quiet convictions we hold so deeply we forget to question them. It colors perception, governs thinking, shapes a life, often working unseen.

It remains our very substance rather than abstraction. The Gita states śraddhāmayo ’yaṁ puruṣo yo yac-chraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ — “a person is what their shraddha is” (17.3). Shraddha stays active and potent, prompting deeds, conditioning behavior, determining how we meet the world.
