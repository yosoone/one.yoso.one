Yes, according to traditional Buddhist accounts, Siddhartha Gautama, who later became known as the Buddha, had a son named Rahula. Siddhartha Gautama was born into a royal family in ancient India, and he renounced his princely life in search of enlightenment.

The story goes that Siddhartha left his palace, family, and worldly possessions in pursuit of spiritual understanding. Before his departure, his wife, Yasodhara (also known as Bhaddakaccana or Princess Yasodhara), was pregnant. After Siddhartha attained enlightenment and became the Buddha, he returned to his homeland. It was during this visit that he met his son Rahula for the first time, who was then seven years old.

The encounter between the Buddha and Rahula is often recounted in Buddhist literature. In one notable episode, Rahula approaches the Buddha and asks for his inheritance. Instead of material wealth, the Buddha provides Rahula with spiritual guidance, emphasizing the importance of truth, ethical conduct, and mindfulness. The Buddha's teachings to Rahula are often encapsulated in the phrase "Rahula, develop a mind like the earth."

Rahula eventually became a monk and played a role in the early Buddhist monastic community. His relationship with the Buddha and the lessons he received from his father are significant elements in the narrative of the Buddha's life and teachings.
