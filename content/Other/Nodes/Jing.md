According to [Traditional Chinese Medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine "Traditional Chinese medicine") theory, _jing_ or _essence_ can be summarised in two parts: the Yin, being congenital or prenatal, and the Yang, being postnatal or acquired. Prenatal _jing_ is acquired at birth from the parents: the father's sperm and the mother's ovum. This is a similar concept to [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA"). Postnatal _jing_ is acquired after birth through food, water, oxygen, as well as environmental and social conditions—very much like the concept of [epigenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics "Epigenetics"). The concept is expounded in the Chinese theoretical cosmological treatise called the [Bagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagua "Bagua") and within the _[I Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching "I Ching")_.

The Yin and Yang _jing_ transform to create and replenish each other. The Yang _jing_ circulates through the eight extraordinary vessels and transforms to become and replenish yin; in turn the marrow becomes blood, body fluid and semen.[[1]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing\_(Chinese\_medicine)#cite\_note-1)

_Jing_ should not be confused with the related concept of _[Neijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neijing "Neijing")_ (勁; "power"), nor with _jīng_ (經; "classic"), which appears in many early Chinese book titles, such as the _[Huangdi Neijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangdi_Neijing "Huangdi Neijing")_, _[Yijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_ching "I ching")_, and _[Chajing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Classic_of_Tea "The Classic of Tea")_.[[250324]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing\_(Chinese\_medicine)#cite\_note-2)

The characteristics which constitute signs of good _jing_ (e.g. facial structure, teeth, hair, strength of [adrenals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland") or [Kidneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_\(Chinese_medicine\) "Kidney (Chinese medicine)")) share the embryological origin of [neural crest cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_crest "Neural crest"). These cells undergo immense and challenging cellular migrations requiring great organisation. As such, _jing_ may simply represent the strength of embryological self-organisation in the organism. This will be manifested most strongly in those cells which require most organisation; that is, the neural crest cells.[[3]]\(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing\_(Chinese\_medicine)#cite\_note-3)\_
