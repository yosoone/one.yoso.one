# The Page You Cannot Edit: Identity as a Contested Record

_An analogy. In the current stack, your identity is a Wikipedia page you are not allowed to edit — and Wikipedia is the working prototype that has been running, in the open, for twenty years._

---

## The central rule

The rule of Wikipedia most people forget: **you are barred from writing your own article.** Conflict-of-interest policy treats the subject as the _least_ trustworthy author of their own entry. Your identity is assembled _about_ you, by others, from approved sources.

That single rule is the whole thesis in miniature:

> The self becomes a record authored by parties other than the self, and your own testimony about who you are is treated as inadmissible.

---

## The mapping

### Neutrality is won, not found

A Wikipedia article wears the banner of **NPOV** — the neutral point of view, the calm consensus, the synthesized truth. But the “neutral” version is simply _whoever prevailed_ in the edit war. Neutrality is an outcome of power dressed as the absence of power.

This is the **filter** again: the single confident answer that looks like reality but is the residue of a fight you never saw. Your identity record carries the same false serenity — the credit score, the verified profile, the official file all read as plain fact, when each is the settled result of contest.

### “Reliable sources” is access control to knowledge

Wikipedia decides truth by citing approved sources — and what counts as _reliable_ is itself gatekept and political. Your own account is dismissed as “original research,” inadmissible. Only credentialed third parties can establish a fact about you.

Onto digital identity: your self-description matters less than what the **verified** sources say — the bank, the state, the platform. You can insist you are who you say you are; the system waits for an approved source to confirm it.

> You are not the author of your record. You are its subject.

### The editors are the competing interests

|Wikipedia editor                                  |Identity-stack equivalent                                                             |
|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|**PR firms / paid editors** sanitizing a page     |Data brokers and advertisers curating the version of you that gets sold               |
|**State actors** editing from government IPs      |National ID, watchlists, credit files — the state’s version of you                    |
|**Ideological factions** warring over framing     |Social reputation, tagging, the public reading of who you are                         |
|**Administrators** with outsized delete/keep power|Infrastructural sovereigns holding the keys; open-looking governance that concentrates|
|**The subject**                                   |You — most invested, least powerful party in the room                                 |

### Citogenesis is the feedback loop made literal

Wikipedia’s signature pathology: an unsourced claim is added, a careless journalist repeats it, and _that article_ becomes the “reliable source” proving the original claim. Error laundered into fact by circular citation.

This is exactly the identity loop:

> The algorithm’s _guess_ about you becomes data, the data trains the next guess, and the inference hardens into a “fact” because the system cited itself.

Your identity gets **citogenized** — predicted, then confirmed by the prediction.

### Notability decides who exists at all

Wikipedia’s harshest gate is whether you are “notable” enough to have a page. Below the line, you do not exist in the record.

The identity stack runs the same gate: the unbanked, the undocumented, those without the right credentials are **non-notable** to the system — legible enough to be excluded, not enough to be served. Existence becomes a permission, granted by whoever defines the threshold.

### Two quieter parallels

- **The edit history never forgets.** Every past version persists, append-only — like a digital identity where nothing is truly deleted and reinvention is foreclosed.
- **Behind every clean article is a furious talk page** — the hidden negotiation the public never reads. The polished front masks the war.

---

## Why it matters

Wikipedia is not merely _like_ the future of identity. It is the **working prototype** — running in the open for two decades — of what happens when a self becomes a contested public record:

- governed by rules you did not write
- edited by interests you cannot see
- gatekept by a hierarchy of “reliable” voices that rarely includes your own
- and presenting the whole apparatus as neutral truth

It is **“possession to permission”** you can click through today. The new stack adds only three things: **scale, automation, and the removal of the talk page** — the contest goes fully invisible.

---

## The closer

> At least a Wikipedia page tells you it is editable and shows you the history.
>
> Your algorithmic identity offers neither the edit button nor the log.
