# Origin of the Seven Chakras

![[Other/Yoga/chakra-systems-diagram.svg]]

_Spelling: this note writes "chakra" throughout, including in Sanskrit compounds (Devīchakra, Ṣaṭchakranirūpaṇa). Published titles keep their original spelling, so Heilijgers-Seelen's book appears as_ Five Cakras.

## Summary

The familiar seven-chakra map is a modern consolidation of a much older and more varied tantric idea. _Chakra_ means "wheel." Medieval tantric texts give four, five, six or more wheels along the central channel, and the number, names and meaning change from text to text. The seven-part list comes from a sixteenth-century Bengali text. Its rainbow colours, glands and psychological traits were added in the West between the 1880s and 1977.

## The systems in historical order

### Buddhist tantra: four chakras

The _Hevajra Tantra_ names four lotuses on the central channel and gives their petal counts in a single verse (I.i.24): 64 at the nirmāṇa chakra, 8 at the dharma chakra, 16 at the sambhoga chakra and 32 at the mahāsukha chakra.[^1] White places such Buddhist hierarchies of inner centres before the Hindu ones.[^2]

| Sanskrit | English | Place | Petals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mahāsukha chakra | chakra of great bliss | head | 32 |
| Sambhoga chakra | chakra of enjoyment | throat | 16 |
| Dharma chakra | chakra of the teaching | heart | 8 |
| Nirmāṇa chakra | chakra of emanation | navel | 64 |

The four chakras correspond to the Buddha's bodies (_kāya_) of emanation, teaching, enjoyment and great bliss. The navel placement is the usual reading, but one translation places the nirmāṇa chakra at the genital region, so check Snellgrove's wording before relying on it.

### Kubjikāmata Tantra: five chakras

The _Kubjikāmata_ ("doctrine of Kubjikā," the "bent" or "hunchbacked" goddess) survives in a critical edition of the Kulālikāmnāya version.[^3] The oldest dated manuscript, of a shorter redaction, is from AD 1037/8, and the text itself is older than that.[^4]

Chapters 14–16 describe five chakras, each the seat of a class of goddesses and, to a lesser degree, of male deities.[^5] Glossary entries based on Heilijgers-Seelen add each chakra's element and body region. They also show that the text names the five in ascending order, from below the navel to above the head.[^6]

| Sanskrit | English | Region | Element |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Khecarīchakra | chakra of the sky-goers | above the head | space |
| Yoginīchakra | chakra of the yoginīs | throat | air |
| Mātṛchakra | chakra of the mothers | heart | fire |
| Dūtīchakra | chakra of the female messengers | navel and just above | water |
| Devīchakra | chakra of the goddesses | below the navel | earth |

The Devīchakra covers the area of the later ādhāra and svādhiṣṭhāna chakras combined.[^6] These chakras are populated by deities. Their element pairing is the earliest element scheme found here, long before the Western one.

### Kubjikāmata Tantra: six chakras

The same text also contains a six-chakra list close to the modern one, which scholars treat as its earliest form.[^7]

| Sanskrit | English |
| --- | --- |
| Ājñā | command |
| Viśuddhi | purification |
| Anāhata | unstruck (sound) |
| Maṇipūra | city of jewels |
| Svādhiṣṭhāna | own base |
| Ādhāra | support |

### Ṣaṭchakranirūpaṇa: six plus the crown

Pūrṇānanda's _Ṣaṭchakranirūpaṇa_ ("Description of the Six Chakras," 1577) fixed the version that became the modern seven.[^8] The title counts six. The thousand-petalled crown sits above them as the culmination, not as a seventh wheel of the same kind.

| Sanskrit | English | Petals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sahasrāra | thousand-petalled | 1000 |
| Ājñā | command | 2 |
| Viśuddha | purified | 16 |
| Anāhata | unstruck (sound) | 12 |
| Maṇipūra | city of jewels | 10 |
| Svādhiṣṭhāna | own base | 6 |
| Mūlādhāra | root support | 4 |

### Modern Western synthesis

Woodroffe (writing as Arthur Avalon) translated the _Ṣaṭchakranirūpaṇa_ and the _Pādukāpañcaka_ ("Fivefold Footstool") as _The Serpent Power_ in 1919.[^8] Kurt Leland traces the Western system in stages:[^9]

- **1880s:** Theosophical writers settle on a base of seven chakras and link each to a nerve plexus.
- **1920s:** English names appear, and each chakra is paired with an endocrine gland.
- **1930s:** Colour therapists assign colours in rainbow order.
- **1977:** Ken Dychtwald's _Bodymind_ first combines the rainbow colours and glands with the familiar list of chakra qualities.

This corrects the common claim that Leadbeater's _The Chakras_ (1927) introduced the rainbow. Leland notes that the Indian texts do give chakras colours, but not in rainbow order.[^9]

## Scientific view

I found no peer-reviewed study establishing chakras as anatomical structures. The positive claims located so far come from non-academic or biofield-therapy journals, which fall outside the source standard. The most careful academic bridge is Joseph Loizzo's review in _Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences_ (2016). It reads the subtle body as a traditional map of nervous-system function and proposes using it as an aid to interoceptive awareness in contemplative practice. It stays under To review because three publisher and index pages blocked access.

Geoffrey Samuel's chapter gives the comparative religious-studies frame for subtle-body systems.[^10]
